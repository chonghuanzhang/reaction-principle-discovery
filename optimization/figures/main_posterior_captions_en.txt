Fig. 5 | Bayesian condition selection on archived reaction screens. Response colours in a,d,e,f–h share one 0–1 relative-response scale: recorded response (a,d,e) or posterior mean (f–h) divided by the highest measured response for that system, using all 96 conditions for S–H and the 60 selected conditions for each N–H/O–H trajectory. Zero is the fixed colour-scale anchor. Relative colours indicate within-system response patterns and do not imply equivalent absolute responses or yields across systems; z axes and numerical cell labels retain raw analytical-response values. a, Measured S–H landscape for 96 base/additive–solvent combinations (12 × 8). Height encodes UV response; point colours give Bayesian selection rounds in one retrospective replay (seed 5010). The star marks PL40-W66 (CsOH/2 in DCE), with a separately recorded 37% NMR yield. Smooth interpolation preserves measured category vertices; UV response is an analytical signal, not percentage yield. b, Fraction of 100 paired runs discovering the maximum recorded S–H response by each completed batch. Each pair shares 12 initial conditions, followed by batches of 12; median first-hit budgets are 24 for Bayesian selection and 48 for random sampling. Shading gives 95% pointwise percentile-bootstrap confidence intervals from 20,000 resamples of paired initialization-seed indices (seed 20261025), conditional on this fixed grid. Bayesian selection uses an upper confidence bound (UCB) acquisition score, μ + 1.96σ, where μ and σ are the posterior latent-response mean and standard deviation. The coefficient 1.96 is a fixed acquisition parameter and does not imply calibrated 95% coverage. c, Paired first-hit budget savings (random minus Bayesian). The mean is 25.8 conditions (gold dashed line; 95% paired percentile-bootstrap interval, 20.9–30.7). Bayesian selection is earlier in 67 pairs, tied in 30 and later in 3. d,e, One paired replay (seed 5010), showing all 96 measured UV responses; central numbers are responses rounded to two decimals, and corner digits are selection rounds. Dotted grey outlines mark the shared initial 12 conditions. Dark-green outlines mark the subsequent 12 Bayesian selections, and dashed grey-green outlines mark the subsequent 36 random selections. Outlines extend through the first completed batch reaching the maximum: 24 Bayesian conditions in two rounds and 48 random conditions in four rounds. Unboxed cells are measured responses unrevealed to that policy at the displayed budget. These are retrospective first-hit endpoints, not stopping decisions. f–h, Final posterior response landscapes after five reconstructed selection rounds for N–H with Ag2SO4/PPh3 (f), O–H with [Cu(MeCN)4]PF6 (g) and O–H with NiCl2·DME (h). Each posterior uses only the 60 selected responses (12 per round) to estimate the latent mean over all 96 candidates. Height encodes posterior mean and colour uses the shared relative-response scale; filled grey points are selected archived measurements, hollow points are posterior means at the other 36 candidates, and stars mark the highest selected measured response. Measured point heights need not lie on the fitted surface. Bounded display interpolation preserves every saved category vertex and does not imply continuous chemical coordinates. N–H retains its recorded MS response units; O–H retains UV response units. These are model estimates, not calibrated yield landscapes; matched numerical NMR yields were not found for the three selected maxima. Uncertainty and round-specific selection maps appear in Repository Figs. BO1–BO2. Round order is computational, without established prospective laboratory dates. All three trajectories reach the 60-condition budget without satisfying the stopping rule (Repository Fig. BO2). Condition keys and model diagnostics are supplied in Repository Figs. BO1–BO3.
