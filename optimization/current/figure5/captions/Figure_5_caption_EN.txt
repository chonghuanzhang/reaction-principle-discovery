Figure 5 | Bayesian condition optimization and representative experimental response landscapes.

a, Optimization trajectories for the N–H, O–H and S–H systems in the shared decision space of 13,920 conditions. Each trajectory contains 12 initial conditions followed by batches of four conditions. Bars show the mean measured UV response within each batch, and whiskers span the minimum to maximum responses among its distinct conditions. Step curves show the highest response obtained up to each round; stars mark the first evaluation of the highest-response condition in each trajectory, and squares mark stopping. Round 0 denotes initialization. UV responses are interpreted within each fixed substrate pair under consistent analytical conditions; absolute signals from different substrate/product systems are not directly comparable.

b–d, Representative 12 × 8 experimental response grids for N–H (b), O–H (c) and S–H (d), assembled from existing measured conditions within the decision space. Each grid combines 12 formulations with eight solvents. N–H and S–H use Ag₂SO₄/PPh₃ and CuCl/PPh₃, respectively, with 12 bases; O–H uses 12 Cu- or Ni-catalyst–base formulations. Surface heights give measured UV responses; colour is normalized to the maximum within each displayed grid, with a separate 0–1 colour bar in each panel. Grey points denote measured conditions, and stars identify the highest response within each grid. Smooth surfaces connect the 96 categorical values for visualization. The N–H grid maximum is distinct from the full-space trajectory maximum in a. Formulation and solvent identities are provided in Supplementary Section S6 and the source data.

e, Evolution of the N–H Gaussian-process posterior after initialization and rounds 1, 2, 3, 10 and 12, arranged from left to right across the two rows. The same 32 conditions are displayed on a fixed, rank-scaled joint coordinate incorporating catalyst–ligand, solvent and base identities. Each posterior uses all observations available at that round: 12, 16, 20, 24, 52 and 60, respectively. Lines denote posterior means, shaded bands indicate ±2 latent posterior standard deviations, and dashed outlines show the band from the immediately preceding round. Blue points show posterior means at evaluated conditions; orange points identify the four conditions evaluated in the indicated round. Stars identify the condition first evaluated in round 10 that gave the highest recorded N–H response. Curves connect discrete condition coordinates for display.
