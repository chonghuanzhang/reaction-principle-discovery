## Frozen Bayesian model and information boundary

Each 12 × 8 grid has 116 feature columns: 12 one-hot base/additive identities, eight one-hot solvent identities and 96 one-hot categorical pairs. Features are centred and divided by their population standard deviation over the unlabelled candidate pool; constant columns use unit scale. The standardized arrays are supplied in `benchmark/data/`. Their ordering is fixed by candidate identifiers.

The implemented finite feature kernel is `K = X X^T`. `model/bo_core.py` evaluates Bayesian linear regression with a normal–inverse-gamma prior, shape 2 and scale 1. There is no extra independent random-intercept variance. The historical protocol's intercept-variance parameter is not used in this kernel implementation. At each update, only revealed responses determine their mean and population SD; a constant observed batch uses unit scale. The posterior is transformed back to the response units. Returned uncertainty is latent-function SD, excluding future-observation noise.

For a selected index set `S`, let `y_S` contain only the revealed responses, `c = mean(y_S)` and `s = std_pop(y_S)`. Replace `s` by 1 when `s <= 1e-12`, and set `z = (y_S - c)/s`. With feature variance factor 1, the posterior is:

```text
K = X X^T
A = I + K_SS
alpha = A^(-1) z
a = 2 + |S|/2
b = 1 + (z^T z - z^T K_SS alpha)/2
mu_x = c + s K_xS alpha
v_x = K_xx - K_xS A^(-1) K_Sx
sigma_x^2 = s^2 [b/(a - 1)] v_x
```

The inverse-gamma prior has shape 2 and scale 1. The implementation clips `v_x` below at zero and evaluates its variance factor as `max(b / max(a - 1, 1e-12), 1e-12)` before taking the square root. Thus `sigma_x` is a latent-function posterior standard deviation, excluding future-observation noise. These numerical guards and the at-most threshold above follow `model/bo_core.py`. Original tests compare this kernel calculation with an independent primal calculation and exercise constant scales and batch-budget boundaries.

The first twelve conditions are sampled uniformly without replacement using Python `random.Random(seed).sample`. Bayesian and random policies share the same ordered initial indices for each paired seed. Subsequent Bayesian batches select the twelve remaining candidates with the largest `mu_x + 1.96 sigma_x + epsilon_x`, where each seeded tie perturbation is uniform in `[0, 1e-12)`. The generator draws one perturbation for each candidate in fixed index order; candidate index resolves any residual score tie. Twelve conditions are selected from a frozen posterior, and the entire selected batch is revealed before the next update. Candidates are never reselected. Random selection shuffles the remaining candidates using its seeded generator and selects the next twelve. The coefficient 1.96 is a fixed acquisition parameter, not a simultaneous 95% guarantee. These are fixed finite-pool policies, rather than a continuous chemical optimization over intermediate base or solvent values. Model configuration was fixed before the recorded computation, but project outcomes and earlier exploratory analyses already existed; this analysis was not prospectively preregistered.

The offline driver owns the complete response grid and reveals only the selected labels after a batch. The `ObservablePolicy`, posterior and stopping monitor receive selected observations; they never receive the full response vector or a known global maximum. Tests change hidden labels and require the current decision and candidate scores to remain unchanged. Catalyst panels and condition grids are evaluated separately; the code does not fabricate an unmeasured cross-product or an end-to-end catalyst-to-grid transition.

## Paired S–H benchmark

The experimental account states that the complete S–H grid was measured before retrospective evaluation. All 96 unique SH_PL40 response records were reconciled to their original workbook cells. The main S–H analysis uses seeds 5000–5099 on this same measured grid, producing 100 paired simulations and 200 trajectories; all trajectories were reconciled to their archived first-hit budgets. Both policies use batches of twelve and a maximum budget of 96; neither simulated strategy creates a new chemical measurement. The endpoint is the first completed batch containing the maximum recorded response, with a response tolerance of 1e-12. Initial conditions count toward the budget. The maximum belongs to the evaluator and is unavailable to the selection policies; this exact-hit metric is not an operational laboratory stopping rule.

Median budgets are 24 conditions for Bayesian selection and 48 for random selection. Mean budgets are 26.04 and 51.84. The paired difference is random minus Bayesian budget: its mean is 25.8 conditions, with a 95% percentile bootstrap interval of 20.88–30.72 based on 20,000 paired-seed resamples and seed 20261025. Bayesian selection is earlier in 67 pairs, tied in 30 and later in three. `SH_100_paired_budgets.csv`, `SH_hit_budget_ecdf.csv` and `SH_reanalysis_summary.json` preserve the current calculations. At a budget of 48 conditions, 100/100 Bayesian runs and 51/100 random runs had found the maximum. For seed `i`, the paired saving is `d_i = B_random,i - B_BO,i`. The percentile bootstrap samples the 100 seed pairs with replacement 20,000 times using NumPy `default_rng(20261025)` and reports the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the mean savings. The pointwise discovery-curve bands use the saved paired-seed bootstrap procedure.

These resamples quantify initialization variation conditional on one fixed historical response grid. They do not estimate wet-laboratory reproducibility, prospective savings or generalization to a different chemistry. The broader historical benchmark retains an earlier 2,000-resample, seed-42 calculation for its eight pools. For the sulfur grid that earlier interval was 20.88–30.603 conditions; its resampling specification must not be attached to the current 20.88–30.72 interval. Its protocol and summaries should not be substituted for the current main S–H bootstrap.

Two 100-seed diagnostics use the same S–H seed range. Additive-feature UCB removes the base–solvent interaction features and gives median budget 24, mean 29.28, and 98 hits by budget 48. The full-feature posterior-mean-only policy gives median 24, mean 26.28, and 100 hits. These equal medians do not isolate a unique contribution from the UCB term or the interaction features. The diagnostic functions in `model/diagnostics.py` preserve these calculations.

Repository Table R3 in `../experiments/bo_benchmark/` provides the readable 100-pair table. `benchmark/data/` additionally retains all eight historical pools: 1,600 complete campaigns, 183,000 selected conditions, 15,800 batch-end trajectory records and complete candidate/observation records for the reference decisions. They describe computational repetition on recorded measurements.

## Selected N–H and O–H trajectories

The fixed seeds are 6000 for NH_PL36, 7000 for OH_PL37 and 8000 for OH_PL38, inherited from reference replicate zero without a seed search. Each trajectory reveals five batches of twelve responses, or 60 per system. The other 36 recorded measurements in each grid remain hidden from the policy, posterior updates and stopping calculations. Repository Table R4 in `../experiments/selected_responses/` provides the 180-row SI table; source-linked rows and stopping checks remain in `selections/`.

The maximum budget and stopping definition were fixed before this recorded stopping-policy computation; they were not prospectively preregistered. The stopping tolerance is fixed after the first batch as δ = 0.10 times that batch's response population SD. A check requires at least 36 selected observations, an improvement in best response over the preceding two batches no greater than δ, and a maximum remaining latent UCB no greater than best observed response plus δ. Two consecutive eligible joint passes are required; the earliest possible model stop is 48 conditions. A constant first batch disables this stopping route. No unselected response or known optimum enters proposal, observation or stopping. A model-rule result remains subject to chemical confirmation; if no model stop occurs, the budget limit is 60.

All three trajectories reach `BUDGET_REACHED_CRITERION_NOT_MET`. Both O–H trajectories have their first joint pass only after batch five, leaving no second consecutive pass before the cap. The factor 1.96 is a heuristic acquisition coefficient, not a calibrated coverage guarantee. δ is an operational tolerance in analytical response units, rather than validated analytical precision or a percentage-point yield difference.

`scripts/reproduce_analysis.py` reruns the saved campaign and stopping definitions, checks ordered selected indices and response values, and reconstructs the posterior after each batch from selected labels only. It compares three pools × five batches × 96 candidates = 1,440 saved posterior rows. Unknown responses remain blank in the posterior table.

The selected files explicitly use `record_type = COMPUTATIONAL_PLAN`, `execution_status = NOT_AN_ACTUAL_BATCH_EXECUTION_RECORD` and blank `actual_lab_batch_date` fields. Their first batch is uniformly random, followed by four frozen-posterior UCB batches; the original screens each contain all 96 measured responses, so the 60 selected labels describe model inputs rather than the total original experiment count. Original immutable recommendation timestamps, dated physical executions, response-return timestamps and linked subsequent recommendations are not supplied by these records. Workbook creation/modification dates, row order and plate identifiers cannot establish that chronology. The absence of these records does not establish that experiments were not sequential; it limits what can be independently verified. These reconstruction seeds and batches must not be assigned to the original laboratory procedure without the corresponding records. No matched prospective N–H/O–H random controls or calibrated per-condition yield series are included. Historical N–H/O–H computational BO/random replays are not wet-laboratory random controls; the present quantitative random-baseline comparison is restricted to SH_PL40. Independent repeats and sample-linked product quantification are needed before calling a selected working condition a chemically validated optimum. Physical preparation, postprocessing, spectra and product characterization are described in the Supporting Information; response tables cannot replace instrument raw files or sample-linked characterization.
