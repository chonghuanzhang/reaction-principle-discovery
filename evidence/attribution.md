# Evidence attribution and chemical transfer

A measured source observation, a mechanistic interpretation and a target-system hypothesis answer different questions. Keeping them separate prevents a plausible analogy from becoming a claimed experiment.

| Evidence | What it supports | What requires an additional argument |
|---|---|---|
| Author-reported observation | The stated outcome under the source's donor, substrate, conditions and endpoint | The same outcome for a new molecular donor or target system |
| Author-proposed explanation | A mechanistic interpretation proposed by the source authors | Direct observation of an unobserved intermediate or a universal pathway |
| Abstract-level report | The information stated in the accessible abstract | Full scope, detailed conditions, missing yields or unavailable control results |
| Metadata-only source | Bibliographic identity | A reaction-support edge, measured failure or absence of prior art |
| Conditional principle application | A chemically specified reason to consider or constrain a candidate | Product formation, reaction yield or independent model-prediction accuracy |

For transfer, compare donor form, acceptor structure, activation regime and endpoint separately. Ph₂PH, Ph₂P(O)H, PhSeH and PhSeSePh are not interchangeable donor identities. Carbonyl interception, C–O–P rearrangement, C–Se coupling, acid-promoted second steps and CF₂-retaining products must retain their own endpoints.

Negative observations remain local to their tested system. An alternative product is evidence of competition rather than no reaction. A 37% phosphorus-containing first-stage product remains positive evidence even if a subsequent hydrofluorination fails. Conversely, missing full text and uncalibrated responses cannot establish target-system failure or a validated optimum.
