# P08 — phosphine oxides, P(O)H

Phosphine oxides react with trifluoromethyl ketones RCOCF3 to give different ketone or enol-phosphinate endpoints. Molecular sieves, reagent loading and temperature change endpoint selection. The acceptor differs from RCOCH2CF3, and the enol-phosphinate linkage is C–O–P rather than direct vinyl C–P.

## Reaction context

| Chemical feature | Source-specific information |
|---|---|
| Donor or intermediate | phosphine oxides, P(O)H |
| Source substrate | trifluoromethyl ketones RCOCF3 |
| Activation | P-centred Pudovik carbonyl addition, proposed phospha-Brook rearrangement and β-fluoride elimination |
| Assessed endpoint | difluoromethyl, monofluoromethyl or methyl ketones; difluoroenol phosphinates |
| Reported outcome | A different fluorination or connectivity endpoint was reported. |
| Conditions | In the model reaction, Ph2P(O)H (1.2 equivalents), K2CO3 (2 equivalents), 1,4-dioxane, 80 °C, 6 h give difluoromethyl ketone in 80%; adding 4 Å molecular sieves affords difluoroenol phosphinate in 80%. Other endpoints require different reagent loading and temperature; see Section S2.5. |

## Interpretation and transfer limits

RCOCF3 differs from RCOCH2CF3, and Ph2P(O)H differs from Ph2PH. The enol phosphinate contains C–O–P. Carbonyl interception and rearrangement are competing-pathway considerations, not direct failure of the project vinyl C–P endpoint.

Initial P-centred addition creates a C–P bond, followed by proposed phosphorus migration to oxygen and elimination. Product connectivity and endpoint must therefore be assessed after the whole sequence.

The source record does not establish an exact match to the carbonyl-retaining RCOCH₂CF₃ → RCOCH=C(F)–Nu target.
