# From literature observations to molecular design

This guide connects reported chemistry to the four reaction principles used to organize candidate design. Start with a principle to understand a conditional relationship, or follow a source quotation to examine the observation behind it.

| Resource | Contents | Main question |
|---|---:|---|
| [Reaction principles](principles/README.md) | 4 cards | What chemical relationship guides a design choice? |
| [Attributed quotations](quotations/README.md) | 43 passages | What did the source report or propose? |
| [Source-specific cases](source_cases/README.md) | 17 cases | Which donor, acceptor, activation and endpoint were tested? |
| [P/Se applicability](applicability/README.md) | 25 records across 9 sources | How far does the source support transfer to the target? |
| [Literature catalog](../literature/README.md) | 370 source records and 403 numbered references | Where does the evidence come from? |

The principles address acceptor activation, donor speciation and capture, pathway divergence after capture, and product persistence. They organize hypotheses; they do not assert that every reaction follows one mechanism or that every candidate forms a product. Read the [attribution guide](attribution.md) before interpreting a negative result or a mechanistic statement.

For data analysis, use [quotation records](quotation_records.csv), [source cases](source_cases.csv), [applicability records](applicability_records.csv), [principle cards](principles.json) and [principle–evidence links](principle_evidence_links.csv). All quoted passages retain attribution and human-readable source locators. Original-language statement data are available in the [literature statement collection](../literature/evidence_statements.csv).

Continue to the [candidate catalog](../candidates/README.md) to inspect molecular structures, proposed reactive forms and product options.
