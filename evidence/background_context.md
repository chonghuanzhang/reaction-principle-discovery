# Background context for monofluoroalkene research

These four source contexts explain the application and selectivity arguments surrounding monofluoroalkenes. The source passages and their limits distinguish demonstrated chemistry, author proposals and possible uses of the present compounds.

## Amide-bond replacements and measured biological activity

Selected monofluoroalkenes have been investigated as peptide amide-bond replacements. In the cited example, fluorinated analogues showed weak to moderate anti-HIV activity but lower potency than SC29EK. This supports an explored medicinal application; it does not establish improved activity or drug status for the present products.

**Source:** [L093](../literature/sources/L093.md) · [repository reference 16](../literature/bibliography.md#ref-16) · [doi:10.3762/bjoc.13.262](https://doi.org/10.3762/bjoc.13.262).

**Location:** Author abstract; publisher full text Introduction and applications.

> Monofluoroalkenes are fluorinated motifs that can be used to replace amide bonds.

**Reported application example:** Weak to moderate anti-HIV activity was observed for the fluorinated analogues, but the potency was always lower than for SC29EK.

<a id="intro-morand"></a>

## Proposed mimics of amide derivatives and phosphates

The authors propose gem-heteroatom-substituted fluoroalkenes as potential stable mimics of specific functional groups. The proposal concerns phosphates, carbamates, S-thiocarbamates and ureas. It does not provide biological validation of the compounds in the present study.

**Source:** [repository reference 17](../literature/bibliography.md#ref-17) · [doi:10.1002/chem.202102548](https://doi.org/10.1002/chem.202102548).

**Location:** Author abstract, Crossref and EuropePMC.

> could be envisioned as stable mimics of various important organic functions, such as phosphates, carbamates, S-thiocarbamates and ureas.

<a id="intro-zeng"></a>

## Selectivity across fluorinated alkene reaction partners

The cited NiH study highlights the need to distinguish the reactivities of an alkene, a gem-difluoroalkene and a monofluoroalkene product during hydronickelation. It provides context for regio-, stereo- and pathway-selectivity questions. Its preformed gem-difluoroalkene coupling partners differ from the saturated CF₃ carbonyl substrates and donor conditions examined here.

**Source:** [L321](../literature/sources/L321.md) · [repository reference 18](../literature/bibliography.md#ref-18) · [doi:10.1038/s41467-024-52054-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52054-6).

**Location:** Introduction, paragraph beginning Recently; primary abstract and Results for successful coupling.

> the initial hydronickelation step must proceed with significantly different reactivities due to the presence of C = C double bonds in the alkene, the gem-difluoroalkene, and the monofluoroalkene product.

## Fluoroalkenyl boron compounds as synthetic building blocks

The reported boryl-group transformations demonstrate the synthetic use of fluoroalkenyl boron building blocks. The source concerns copper-catalysed borylation of polyfluoroalkenes. It does not directly establish the present RCOCH₂CF₃/heteroatom transformation, and an atorvastatin-mimic example does not establish broad medicinal deployment.

**Source:** [L073](../literature/sources/L073.md) · [repository reference 19](../literature/bibliography.md#ref-19) · [doi:10.1021/jacs.7b08343](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.7b08343).

**Location:** Author abstract, EuropePMC PMID28849929.

> Further transformation of the boryl group has allowed facile preparation of fluoroalkene derivatives
