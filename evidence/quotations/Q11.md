# Q11 — Thiolate addition can compete through fluoride elimination

## Chemical claim

In their summary of prior literature, the authors describe thiolate addition to gem-difluoroalkenes as generating an anionic intermediate that can readily undergo β-fluoride elimination, competing with fluorine-retaining hydrothiolation.

**Attribution:** Author mechanistic summary of prior literature.

**Source:** [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md). [doi:10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343)

**Location:** 10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343; Physical PDF p. 2; Introduction; known competing defluorination pathway.

## Source passage

> In the forward reaction, the σ-withdrawing character of the fluorine atoms would activate the alkene for nucleophilic attack by the thiol.5,6,9–11 However, in practice, nucleophilic addition of a thiolate into gem-difluoroalkenes generates an anionic intermediate 2 that can readily undergo β-fluoride elimination.6,10 This elimination has been partially overcome using several different strategies (Figure 1B),5 such as kinetically quenching the anionic intermediate 2 with molecular oxygen,12 exploiting an acidic organocatalyst,8,13 or trapping the anion with an appended electrophile.14 However, these strategies only function on styrenyl gem-difluoroalkenes, which react through stabilized benzylic carbanion intermediates.

The passage supports the attributed source claim. Applying it to another donor, substrate, activation regime or product endpoint requires a separate chemical argument.
