# Q12 — Additives select difluoroalkyl versus monofluorovinyl thioethers

## Chemical claim

An aniline additive increases formation of the difluorinated thioether, whereas K2CO3 promotes the competing monofluorovinyl thioether and lowers chemoselectivity under the tested conditions.

**Attribution:** Author-reported observation.

**Source:** [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md). [doi:10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343)

**Location:** 10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343; Physical PDF p. 3; Results and discussion; additive control experiments.

## Source passage

> Control experiments indicate that the aniline additives facilitate formation of the α,α−difluorinated thioether product (21, Figure 4A; S6), which contrasts previously reported conditions for defluorinative thiolation that utilized K2CO3 as an additive (Figure 2C).22 As a control, the reaction of gem-difluorostyrene 1 with octane-1-thiol proceeds in 45% yield and 19:1 selectivity in the absence of an additive (entry 1), while the addition of 10% p-aminoacetophenone increases the yield to 68% and selectivity beyond 25:1 (entry 2). In contrast, addition of K2CO3 (2.0 equiv.) increases the yield of undesired Sorrentino et al. α-fluorovinylthioether 25 and decreases the selectivity of the reaction to 2:1 (entry 3), while the addition of both p-aminoacetophenone and K2CO3 provides yield and selectivity comparable to the control reaction that lacks both additives (entry 4 vs. 1).

The passage supports the attributed source claim. Applying it to another donor, substrate, activation regime or product endpoint requires a separate chemical argument.
