# Q13 — Proposed roles of aniline in photocatalytic hydrothiolation

## Chemical claim

The authors propose that the aniline additive may mediate redox events or act as a proton-coupled electron-transfer co-catalyst and hydrogen-atom donor, while explicitly noting the absence of robust mechanistic support.

**Attribution:** Author-proposed hypothesis.

**Source:** [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md). [doi:10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343)

**Location:** 10.1021/acs.joc.2c02343; Physical PDF p. 4; Results and conclusion; proposed role of aniline and stated mechanistic uncertainty.

## Source passage

> At this point, we speculate that the aniline additive might mediate the redox events between the Ir-based catalyst and the thiol substrate, and also serve a role as an H• donor to quench radical intermediates (Figure 2B).25,26 A range of aliphatic gem-difluoroalkenes successfully coupled with 3-mercaptophenol (26) to afford α,α-difluoroalkylthioethers in good to excellent yields (Figure 5). Further, this reaction exploits an underutilized aniline- derived additive as a possible redox mediator and/or PCET co-catalyst, enhancing fluorine retentive hydrofunctionalization of gem-difluoroalkenes. The reported reaction contrasts related conditions that utilize K2CO3 that undergo a SET to generate anion 18 prior to β-fluoride elimination (Figure 2C).22 However, robust support for aniline’s role does not exist in the literature. Mechanistic studies of this iridium and aniline co-catalyzed reaction are underway and will be reported separately in due course.
