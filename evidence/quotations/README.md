# Attributed chemical quotations

These 43 records pair a chemical claim with its exact source passage and locator. Author observations and proposed explanations retain their original attribution. A quotation establishes what a source reports, not experimental verification in the present target system.

- [Q01](./Q01.md) · [L088](../../literature/sources/L088.md) · **Author-reported observation**. After addition of one equivalent of isopropylmagnesium chloride, 19F NMR monitoring detected a β,β-difluoroacrylamide intermediate at −76.0 and −71.2 ppm. Addition of the second equivalent produced the (E)-fluoroalkene 8.

- [Q02](./Q02.md) · [L088](../../literature/sources/L088.md) · **Author-proposed hypothesis**. Because the proposed enolate was not detected by 19F NMR, the authors suggested that fluoride elimination from this intermediate is instantaneous.

- [Q03](./Q03.md) · [L088](../../literature/sources/L088.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Phenyllithium in the presence of TMEDA gave the double-addition product in 75% yield; the ketone was reported exclusively as the (Z)-fluoroalkene. The authors state that TMEDA improved selectivity and yield.

- [Q04](./Q04.md) · [L088](../../literature/sources/L088.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Addition of commonly used Grignard reagents to morpholine 3,3,3-trifluoropropanamide affords (E)-β-fluoro-α,β-unsaturated amides with high stereocontrol.

- [Q05](./Q05.md) · [L294](../../literature/sources/L294.md) · **Author-proposed hypothesis**. The authors propose that oxyanion substitution proceeds by 1,4-addition followed by fluoride expulsion from a metal-enolate intermediate.

- [Q06](./Q06.md) · [L294](../../literature/sources/L294.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Under the reported conditions, the branched alkoxides examined reacted more slowly than the primary alkoxides examined.

- [Q07](./Q07.md) · [L294](../../literature/sources/L294.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Sodium and lithium alkoxides displaced vinylic fluorine in difluorobutenolides 1 and 2 with complete regioselectivity at the β-carbon, affording products 4–10.

- [Q08](./Q08.md) · [L294](../../literature/sources/L294.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Methylmagnesium bromide and phenylmagnesium bromide, modified with a copper(I) bromide–dimethyl sulfide complex, reacted with butenolide 1 exclusively at the β-position to give C-substituted products 25 and 26. Carbonyl-addition products were not observed.

- [Q09](./Q09.md) · [L294](../../literature/sources/L294.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Methyllithium and phenyllithium gave rearranged products 27 and 28. These products lacked the characteristic carbonyl absorption of the starting butenolide ring and contained a ketone group.

- [Q10](./Q10.md) · [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md) · **Author-reported observation**. The authors report photocatalytic hydrothiolation of gem-difluoroalkenes to afford α,α-difluoroalkyl thioethers.

- [Q11](./Q11.md) · [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md) · **Author mechanistic summary of prior literature**. In their summary of prior literature, the authors describe thiolate addition to gem-difluoroalkenes as generating an anionic intermediate that can readily undergo β-fluoride elimination, competing with fluorine-retaining hydrothiolation.

- [Q12](./Q12.md) · [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md) · **Author-reported observation**. An aniline additive increases formation of the difluorinated thioether, whereas K2CO3 promotes the competing monofluorovinyl thioether and lowers chemoselectivity under the tested conditions.

- [Q13](./Q13.md) · [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md) · **Author-proposed hypothesis**. The authors propose that the aniline additive may mediate redox events or act as a proton-coupled electron-transfer co-catalyst and hydrogen-atom donor, while explicitly noting the absence of robust mechanistic support.

- [Q14](./Q14.md) · [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md) · **Author-reported observation and mechanistic inference**. The difluorinated thioether remained stable under the tested K2CO3 conditions. The authors therefore exclude it as an intermediate leading to the monofluorovinyl product under those conditions.

- [Q15](./Q15.md) · [L104](../../literature/sources/L104.md) · **Author-reported observation**. The tested primary and secondary alkyl thiols gave products in 82–95% yield. Two tertiary thiols gave the corresponding products in 87% and 39% yield.

- [Q16](./Q16.md) · [L223](../../literature/sources/L223.md) · **Author-reported observation**. The reaction conditions accommodated boronic acids and the reported potassium organoboron salts. Boronic esters reacted sluggishly, and arylstannanes and arylsilanes were inert both with and without added fluoride.

- [Q17](./Q17.md) · [L223](../../literature/sources/L223.md) · **Author-reported observation**. The reaction generally favoured monocoupled products. For 3m, lowering the boronic-acid loading to near-stoichiometric quantities suppressed overcoupling and allowed isolation of 3m in moderate yield.

- [Q18](./Q18.md) · [L223](../../literature/sources/L223.md) · **Author-reported observation**. A difluoroacrylate mechanistic probe gave a mixture of defluorocoupled product 6 and 1,4-conjugate-addition product 7.

- [Q19](./Q19.md) · [L223](../../literature/sources/L223.md) · **Author-reported observation**. When 1,1-difluoro-1,2-diphenylethane 8 was subjected to the standard conditions, monofluoroalkene 9 was not observed.

- [Q20](./Q20.md) · [L189](../../literature/sources/L189.md) · **Author-reported observation**. The authors report that further increasing the amount of PhSiH3 can remove the third fluorine from some substrates bearing electron-withdrawing groups.

- [Q21](./Q21.md) · [L189](../../literature/sources/L189.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Diazaphospholene-catalysed hydrodefluorination of trifluoromethylalkenes gives gem-difluoroalkenes or terminal monofluoroalkenes, with chemoselectivity controlled by reactant stoichiometry.

- [Q22](./Q22.md) · [L189](../../literature/sources/L189.md) · **Author-reported observation and mechanistic inference**. Stoichiometric and NMR experiments detected a hydrophosphination intermediate. Heating to 70 °C converted this intermediate to the mono-hydrodefluorination product and a fluorinated phosphorus species, supporting a stepwise addition/β-fluoride-elimination pathway.

- [Q23](./Q23.md) · [L133](../../literature/sources/L133.md) · **Author mechanistic summary of prior literature**. The authors summarize a polar addition/elimination pathway in which nucleophilic addition to gem-difluoroalkenes under basic conditions gives anionic intermediates that undergo β-fluoride elimination. They cite prior primary literature, including Peng et al. (2019).

- [Q24](./Q24.md) · [L357](../../literature/sources/L357.md) · **Author-abstract report**. The author abstract reports that gem-difluorostyrenes react with diphenylphosphine oxide or a reagent described as “dialkyl phosphate” in the presence of DBU at room temperature to give monofluorovinyl phosphorus products.

- [Q25](./Q25.md) · [L133](../../literature/sources/L133.md) · **Author-reported observation**. For p-MeO-C6H4–CH=CF2 and Ph2P(O)H (3 equivalents), 5 mol% di-tert-butyl peroxide in tBuOH at 120 °C for 16 h under N2 gave >95% 19F NMR yield in optimization. The scope experiment for product 3a gave 90% isolated yield, averaged over two reactions conducted for 24–26 h.

- [Q26](./Q26.md) · [L133](../../literature/sources/L133.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Nitro-substituted gem-difluoroalkenes underwent fluorine-elimination processes both with and without peroxide under the examined conditions.

- [Q27](./Q27.md) · [L133](../../literature/sources/L133.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Diethyl phosphite gave only trace hydrophosphinylation product 4g, and further optimization was unsuccessful. Di-n-pentylphosphine oxide gave 4f in 50% yield, whereas ethyl phenylphosphinate gave 4e in 29% yield with 20 mol% peroxide.

- [Q28](./Q28.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Hydrofluorination of fluorovinyl ether 3 was ineffective with NEt3·3HF alone. Adding MsOH gave 83% yield of the gem-difluorinated ether with substantial side-product formation; using TFA as the coacid in toluene gave 99% yield.

- [Q29](./Q29.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Phenol-derived fluorovinyl ethers required the stronger coacid MsOH for subsequent hydrofluorination, whereas fluorovinyl alkyl thioethers required pyridine·HF with a CuCl additive.

- [Q30](./Q30.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Diphenylphosphine reacted with (4,4,4-trifluorobutoxy)methylbenzene 1 to form a monofluorovinyl phosphine in a reported yield of 37%.

- [Q31](./Q31.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Subsequent hydrofluorination of the monofluorovinyl phosphine gave a complex product mixture, with no desired phosphorus-substituted gem-difluoride detected.

- [Q32](./Q32.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. N-Methylaniline gave the fluoroenamine in 82% yield using NaHMDS in N,N-dimethylacetamide. Piperidine did not give the fluorinated enamine under the examined conditions, and poor mass balance was observed alongside the gem-difluoroalkene intermediate.

- [Q33](./Q33.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. The N-derived monofluorinated enamine was accessible, but its subsequent hydrofluorination was unsuccessful under several conditions optimized for fluorovinyl ether hydrofluorination.

- [Q34](./Q34.md) · [L344](../../literature/sources/L344.md) · **Author-reported observation**. In the NiH-catalysed hydroselenylation of nonfluorinated N-phenylbut-3-enamide, PhSeH did not deliver the desired β-selenide. Under the compared conditions, PhSeSePh gave 91% 1H NMR yield, an 18:1 regioisomeric ratio and a 97:3 enantiomeric ratio.

- [Q35](./Q35.md) · [L344](../../literature/sources/L344.md) · **Author-reported observation**. With PhSeSePh as the selenium source, omitting the BnBr scavenger lowered the product yield from 91% to 11% by 1H NMR.

- [Q36](./Q36.md) · [L344](../../literature/sources/L344.md) · **Author-reported observation and mechanistic inference**. A preparative reaction using 1.0 mmol of alkene gave BnSePh, consistent with interception of a nucleophilic selenium species generated during diselenide activation.

- [Q37](./Q37.md) · [L359](../../literature/sources/L359.md) · **Author-abstract report**. The author abstract reports nickel-catalysed reductive cross-coupling of gem-difluoroalkenes with selenosulfonates to give selenylated monofluoroalkenes through C–F cleavage and C–Se bond formation.

- [Q38](./Q38.md) · [L346](../../literature/sources/L346.md) · **Author-reported observation**. Benzeneselenol gave PhSe–CH=CF2 in 66% yield under the TFHZ-Tfs-derived coupling conditions. This product retains two fluorines, with selenium attached to the other vinyl carbon.

- [Q39](./Q39.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author mechanistic interpretation**. The authors describe a base-promoted dehydrofluorination/nucleophilic addition–elimination sequence that produces monofluorovinyl intermediates, followed by hydrofluorination to gem-difluorinated products.

- [Q40](./Q40.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation and proposed explanation**. Piperidine did not form the fluorinated enamine intermediate, and poor mass balance was observed alongside the gem-difluoroalkene. The authors tentatively attribute this result to the low acidity of alkylamines.

- [Q41](./Q41.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation**. A tertiary-alcohol example gave the fluorovinyl ether in 51% yield. Subsequent hydrofluorination gave only 4% yield by 19F NMR and poor mass balance, distinguishing successful monofluorovinyl-intermediate formation from inefficient formation of the final gem-difluoride.

- [Q42](./Q42.md) · [L350](../../literature/sources/L350.md) · **Author-reported observation and proposed explanation**. The tested diol gave fluorovinyl ether signals corresponding to 28% yield by 19F NMR and poor mass balance. The authors propose that additional hydrogen bonding lowers nucleophilicity.

- [Q43](./Q43.md) · [L360](../../literature/sources/L360.md) · **Author-abstract report and mechanistic interpretation**. The author abstract reports cyanation and trifluoromethylation of gem-difluoroalkenes with trimethylsilyl nucleophiles to give functionalized monofluoroalkenes. The authors attribute the catalytic role of Cs2CO3 to maintaining a sufficient concentration of CN− or CF3− to initiate the catalytic cycle.
