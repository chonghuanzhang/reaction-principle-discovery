# RP4 — Selective formation must outpace downstream consumption

When the first fluorinated product remains reactive, greater reagent exposure or stronger downstream activation can consume it by further coupling, hydrofluorination or additional C–F loss. Product accumulation therefore depends on a window that permits initial capture while suppressing subsequent transformation. This relation applies only when the downstream reaction is accessible; source-proven persistence is an explicit exception.

## Directional chemical implications

### Increase excess donor/reductant or activate a documented downstream product reaction

Increase secondary product consumption relative to formation. Lower accumulation of the intended fluorination state even if starting-material conversion improves.

### Reduce the reagent exposure that causes verified secondary reaction

Suppress overcoupling or further defluorination. Can recover selective accumulation without changing the initial donor class.

### Change electron donation of the newly attached heteroatom group

Change susceptibility of a fluorovinyl product to protonation and subsequent addition. Product survival must be evaluated separately for N-, O- and S-linked targets.

## Source scope

- Thornbury/Toste: one susceptible monofluorostilbene gives overcoupled products; near-stoichiometric boronic acid remedies that case.
- Zhang/Yang/Cheng: hydrosilane stoichiometry controls mono/di-HDF; further PhSiH3 can remove the third fluoride from some electron-poor substrates.
- Coradi/Bandar: O-vinyl products undergo intentional acid-dependent hydrofluorination; N/P intermediates form but fail the subsequent source CF2 endpoint.
- Sorrentino: isolated CF2 thioether is stable to K2CO3 under the tested conditions, excluding that product as the elimination intermediate.

## Mechanistic status

Observed secondary reactions and resubjection tests in source systems; not evidence that all project monofluoroalkenes are kinetic intermediates or that stronger base universally over-defluorinates.

## Implications for candidate design

- Retain electronic and donor-form diversity to probe both formation and persistence; do not rank all candidates by initial nucleophilicity alone.
- Record alternative endpoints and recovery risks with every proposed molecule.
- Treat coordinating donors and scavenger effects as system-specific compatibility tests; source inhibition can justify an individual negative control but not an element-wide exclusion.

## Representative candidates

- **[C003](../../candidates/C003.md), [C020](../../candidates/C020.md) — 4-pyridylboronic acid / 2,6-dimethylphenylboronic acid.** Electronics, steric effects and coordination can change formation and subsequent coupling; source-specific evaluation remains required.
- **[C051](../../candidates/C051.md), [C064](../../candidates/C064.md) — phenol / sodium phenoxide.** O-donor formulation and fluorovinyl-product fate are separate questions.
- **[C095](../../candidates/C095.md), [C100](../../candidates/C100.md) — diphenylphosphine / benzeneselenol.** Selected unfavorable cases can be negative trials; positive P/Se precedents and incomplete project condition records prohibit class-wide rejection.

## Limits and exceptions

- Stable fluorinated products need not be transient stopping points.
- Failure of source hydrofluorination is not failure to form the monofluoro intermediate sought here.
- Changes in salt, water or base may alter donor/catalyst speciation rather than product-consumption rates.

## Source-specific applicability constraints

- [L343-01](../applicability/L343-01.md): Ph2P(O)H (1.2 equiv) and K2CO3 (2 equiv), 1,4-dioxane, 80 °C, 6 h convert model RCOCF3 to RCOCHF2 in 80% yield. With 4 Å molecular sieves, DFEP 2Aa is obtained in 80% yield. The change concerns endpoint selection, not disappearance of P reactivity. Conditional carbonyl-interception, rearrangement and endpoint-selection constraints; no direct failure of the project target.
- [L343-02](../applicability/L343-02.md): 2.5 equiv Ph2P(O)H at 130 °C gives monofluoromethyl ketone 4A in 67%; 4 equiv at 160 °C gives methyl ketone 6A in 47%. A monofluoromethyl ketone is not a monofluorovinyl C–P product. Conditional carbonyl-interception, rearrangement and endpoint-selection constraints; no direct failure of the project target.
- [L343-03](../applicability/L343-03.md): The final primary text reports failure and recovery for strongly electron-withdrawing substituted trifluoromethyl ketones. Individual identities and yields are not assigned in that paragraph. Hydration-related interference is the authors’ mechanistic explanation; the text calls the hydrate a hemiacetal, which should not be silently endorsed as exact structural terminology. Do not assign this failure to the project electrophile. Conditional carbonyl-interception, rearrangement and endpoint-selection constraints; no direct failure of the project target.
- [L343-04](../applicability/L343-04.md): Separately prepared protonated phospha-Brook side product 5A gives no 3A under K2CO3/80 °C conditions. This distinguishes an unproductive trapped intermediate from the productive anionic elimination sequence; it is not a failed addition of Ph2PH to project substrate A. Route-level intermediate-competence constraint: isolated 5A does not furnish 3A in the stated test. No direct test of C090 donor activity or project target failure.
- [L343-05](../applicability/L343-05.md): With Ph2P(O)H the monofluoroenol phosphinate could not be isolated and monofluoromethyl ketone formed instead. Diethyl phosphite and p-methoxy diarylphosphine oxide provide isolable enol phosphinates. The relevant retained phosphorus linkage is C–O–P; inability to isolate it cannot establish inability to form direct vinyl C–P. Conditional carbonyl-interception, rearrangement and endpoint-selection constraints; no direct failure of the project target.
- [L343-06](../applicability/L343-06.md): Under condition A, competing RCOCF3 reacts while difluoromethyl ketone 3A is recovered in 84% and 4A is not formed. Under stronger conditions the same class can proceed further. Failure is therefore condition/endpoint specific. Conditional carbonyl-interception, rearrangement and endpoint-selection constraints; no direct failure of the project target.
- [L343-07](../applicability/L343-07.md): The authors propose P-centred Pudovik addition, phospha-Brook migration of phosphorus from carbon to oxygen, beta-fluoride elimination and HF-dependent endpoint selection. Mechanistic rationale for a possible competing pathway, not an observed target-system mechanism.
- [L344-01](../applicability/L344-01.md): PhSeH failed to give the desired beta-selenide in the specified nonfluorinated NiH reaction; no calibrated zero yield is assigned. Donor/catalyst compatibility and turnover constraint; not a project-specific negative observation.
- [L344-02](../applicability/L344-02.md): Diphenyl diselenide furnished the beta-selenide in 91% NMR yield under the compared NiH conditions. Donor/catalyst compatibility and turnover constraint; not a project-specific negative observation.
- [L344-03](../applicability/L344-03.md): Omitting BnBr reduced the reported NMR yield from 91% to 11%; this is a condition effect, not absence of C–Se chemistry. Donor/catalyst compatibility and turnover constraint; not a project-specific negative observation.
- [L344-04](../applicability/L344-04.md): The authors attribute PhSeH incompatibility to coordinating selenolate/off-cycle nickel species; the detailed causal mechanism is an interpretation. Donor/catalyst compatibility and turnover constraint; not a project-specific negative observation.
- [L345-01](../applicability/L345-01.md): PhSeH gives the nonfluorinated selenylative alkoxycarbonylation product in 88% GC yield under optimized Pd/CO conditions. Condition-specific compatibility concern with explicit positive counterevidence.
- [L345-02](../applicability/L345-02.md): Added bases including Na2CO3 and Et3N suppress catalytic activity; DMF and DMSO give negligible product in this Pd/CO reaction. Condition-specific compatibility concern with explicit positive counterevidence.
- [L345-03](../applicability/L345-03.md): Dimethyl diselenide gives less than 5% product under standard conditions, distinct from the productive PhSeH donor. Condition-specific compatibility concern with explicit positive counterevidence.

- [L347-02](../applicability/L347-02.md): Benzimidazole affords the CF2-retaining product in 87%; several azaindoles instead furnish monofluorovinyl products. Competing donor roles and fluorination-endpoint control, without assigning target PhSeH failure.

Competing donor roles and fluorination-endpoint control, without assigning target PhSeH failure.
- [L370-01](../applicability/L370-01.md): Ph2PH gives no desired coupling with the tested nonfluorinated terminal allene. Local donor and acceptor controls support conditional caution while retaining successful P–H chemistry.
- [L370-02](../applicability/L370-02.md): Cy2PH and tBu2PH showed no reactivity under varied conditions; substrate inhibition is the authors' proposed explanation. Local donor and acceptor controls support conditional caution while retaining successful P–H chemistry.
- [L370-03](../applicability/L370-03.md): The optimized gem-difluoroallene/Ph2PH reaction affords product in 83% isolated yield after oxidation; the source reports a CF2-retaining allylic phosphorus product. Local donor and acceptor controls support conditional caution while retaining successful P–H chemistry.
- [L350-01](../applicability/L350-01.md): Ph2PH affords a fluorovinyl phosphine in 37% yield before unsuccessful subsequent HF addition. The first-stage C–P product is positive evidence in its reported scaffold. Mandatory counterevidence to phosphorus-wide exclusion; direct target transfer remains conditional.

These principle cards express evidence-based conditional relationships. They do not establish a common mechanism for every substrate or a measured outcome for every proposed candidate.
