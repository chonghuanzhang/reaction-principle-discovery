# RP3 - The state formed by capture controls fluoride loss versus fluorine retention

Where capture generates a suitably configured anionic or metal-associated β-fluoro intermediate, β-fluoride elimination can produce a monofluoroalkene. In compatible radical-addition systems, hydrogen-atom termination can instead trap a saturated CF2 product. Conditions that increase anionic elimination relative to H-termination can therefore improve the monofluoro branch even when they lower the yield of a competing hydroaddition product.

## Directional chemical implications

### Favor anionic/metal-associated addition with accessible β-F geometry

Enable fluoride expulsion before protonation or other termination. Favor a fluorinated alkene over a fluorine-retaining saturated adduct, only in systems with that intermediate.

### Favor radical capture followed by efficient H-atom donation

Quench the radical without fluoride loss. Can improve C–heteroatom capture while diverting from the monofluoroalkene target.

## Source scope

- Carbon: Grignard trapping after observed difluoroacrylamide formation; enolate/F-elimination sequence partly inferred.
- Oxygen: difluorobutenolide oxyanion substitution is interpreted as addition then fluoride expulsion.
- Sulfur: Sorrentino et al. directly change additive effects; K2CO3 increases fluorovinylthioether relative to CF2 hydrothiolation, whereas an aniline additive favors CF2 product.
- Phosphorus provides an additional source-specific contrast between polar phosphinylation and peroxide-initiated hydrophosphinylation, not the organizing axis.

## Mechanistic status

Product branching is experimentally observed in individual sources. Aniline HAT/PCET role and several local intermediates are source hypotheses; no universal mechanism is asserted.

## Candidate-generation rules

- Retain both deprotonatable donors and radical precursors as differentiated hypotheses with explicit fluorine-count endpoints.
- For each donor require a target C–Nu connection and distinguish monofluoroalkene, CF2 addition product, full defluorination and carbonyl addition.
- Do not classify a radical donor as impossible: reduction to an anion can restore elimination in a different catalytic setting, which must be separately supported.

## Representative recommendations

- ** - thiophenol / sodium thiophenolate.** Same S fragment can enter radical or polar chemistry; terminal fluorine count is part of the route hypothesis.
- ** - methanol.** O-capture on a verified fluorinated acceptor can lead to an addition–elimination product; proton competition remains condition-dependent.
- ** - NHPI acetate.** A retained radical-precursor boundary candidate, not a predicted successful monofluoroalkene donor.

## Limits and exceptions

- Nitro-substituted gem-difluoroalkenes undergo fluoride elimination even in peroxide conditions in the P(O)H source.
- Not every radical route retains CF2 and not every polar addition eliminates F.
- Capture on a gem-difluoroalkene establishes only a local step, not two-F loss from the saturated project substrate.

These conditional relationships guide candidate generation; they do not establish a common mechanism or a measured outcome for every recommendation.
