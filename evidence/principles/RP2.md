# RP2 — Accessible donor speciation and capture competence must coincide

Deprotonation or unmasking can raise the population of a bond-forming donor, but resonance stabilization, steric shielding, ion pairing and competing reactive atoms can lower or redirect its capture. Thus, a more basic neutral donor need not be more effective in a deprotonation-dependent reaction, and changing a precursor can change performance without changing the transferred element.

## Directional chemical implications

### Use a donor that can form the required anion under the available base/medium

Increase effective nucleophile availability. Can enable capture when the neutral donor does not react.

### Increase resonance delocalization or steric shielding at the reactive atom

May improve acidity while decreasing intrinsic capture or shifting the favored site. Choose contrasting electronic/steric variants, not a single pKa or elemental ranking.

### Switch neutral donor to salt, organometallic or silylated precursor

Change active-species generation, counterion and competing chemistry. Treat precursor forms as distinct candidates with distinct activation conditions.

## Source scope

- Coradi/Bandar: N-methylaniline gives fluoroenamine whereas piperidine fails under the examined base-promoted conditions; acidity rationale is proposed, not experimentally isolated.
- Paleta et al.: branched alkoxides react more slowly than primary alkoxides on difluorobutenolides; organolithium and Cu-modified Grignard donors give different sites/pathways.
- Thornbury/Toste: boronic-acid/boron-salt transfer differs from sluggish boronic esters and inert arylstannanes/arylsilanes in one Pd system.
- Jiang et al. author abstract: Cs2CO3 with TMSCN/TMSCF3 maintains nucleophilic anions.

## Mechanistic status

Source-specific observations support precursor-, steric- and site-dependence; cross-class coupling of speciation and capture is a qualitative synthesis, not a measured universal rate law.

## Implications for candidate design

- Enumerate neutral/salt/latent-precursor pairs: methanol/alkoxide, phenol/phenoxide, aniline/anilide, thiol/thiolate, and silylated carbon donors.
- Span localized, aryl-delocalized, aromatic and carbonyl/sulfonyl-delocalized N donors; retain uncertain amides and sulfonamides with explicit activation questions.
- Vary aryl electronics and steric bulk; retain ambident C/O and C/N alternatives with atom-specific targets.

## Representative candidates

- **[C048](../../candidates/C048.md), [C045](../../candidates/C045.md) — aniline / lithium anilide.** Neutral/anion pair separates donor availability from structural identity.
- **[C061](../../candidates/C061.md), [C060](../../candidates/C060.md), [C063](../../candidates/C063.md) — methanol / sodium methoxide / lithium methoxide.** Precursor and counterion variation, without assuming the salts are universally superior.
- **[C012](../../candidates/C012.md), [C002](../../candidates/C002.md) — sodium dimethyl malonate / silyl ketene acetal.** Different C-nucleophile formulations probe anion access and activation compatibility.
- **[C011](../../candidates/C011.md), [C027](../../candidates/C027.md) — lithium acetone enolate / pyrrole.** Preserve C/O or C/N capture alternatives instead of a forced elemental label.

## Limits and exceptions

- Acidity alone does not isolate the aniline/piperidine contrast.
- Branched O-donor effects on cyclic difluorobutenolides do not establish a universal steric penalty: tertiary thiols work in a distinct photochemical hydrothiolation.
- TMSCN/TMSCF3 evidence does not validate TMSN3, all silyl enol ethers, or all silylated donors.
