# Tier 4 — Competing pathways, catalyst compatibility and failure boundaries

This chemical-relevance category contains 42 source records. A tier is not a source-quality ranking and does not imply a uniform access level.

- [L369 — Selective oxidation of hydroxy groups of phenylthio and phenylseleno alcohols](../sources/L369.md). The verified title concerns selective hydroxy-group oxidation in phenylthio- and phenylseleno-substituted alcohols. The accessible record does not establish a failed reaction of free PhSeH, C–F cleavage or monofluorovinyl C–Se formation. Tier 4 is a provisional potential role for oxidative compatibility, subject to full-text confirmation. No target-negative observation or RP-support edge is assigned.

- [L344 — NiH‐Catalyzed Enantio‐ and Regioselective Hydroselenylation of Unactivated Alkenes](../sources/L344.md). PhSeH gives no desired β-selenide in NiH-catalysed hydroselenylation of nonfluorinated N-phenylbut-3-enamide, whereas PhSeSePh gives 91% 1H NMR yield. Removing BnBr lowers the yield to 11%. The authors interpret these effects through selenium-source activation and interception of strongly coordinating selenolate. This is direct donor- and additive-dependent evidence within the reported NiH system, without a calibrated zero yield for PhSeH. It supports a compatibility question for the target reaction, not a universal Se exclusion or a direct test of RCOCH2CF3.

- [L345 — Palladium-Catalyzed Selenylative Alkoxycarbonylation of Alkynes toward β-Selenated Cinnamic Acid Esters](../sources/L345.md). PhSeH is productive in Pd-catalysed selenylative alkoxycarbonylation of phenylacetylene, ethanol and CO: the optimized reaction gives 88% GC yield. Added bases lower the reported yield to 35% with Na2CO3, 33% with EtONa, 28% with t-BuOK and 22% with Et3N; DMF and DMSO give negligible product. Dimethyl diselenide gives less than 5%, whereas PhSeSePh affords the carbonylated product in 91%. These observations show donor-form and condition dependence. Formation of the productive diselenide cannot by itself be treated as catalyst deactivation. The reaction does not test a fluorinated carbonyl electrophile.

- [L242 — Farewell to the HSAB Treatment of Ambident Reactivity](../sources/L242.md). For thiocyanate, the review summarizes that kinetically controlled reactions generally yield products of S attack, whereas thermodynamically controlled reactions generally yield products of N attack. For enolates, the site of electrophilic attack depends on enolate structure, electrophile, solvent, and counterion, so selectivity cannot be assigned from nucleophile identity alone. The review reports that additions of nucleophiles to sp2 carbon electrophiles generally have lower intrinsic barriers than SN2 substitutions, where bond cleavage in the rate-determining step requires more structural reorganization.

- [L010 — Palladium‐Catalyzed C–S Bond Formation of Stable Enamines with Arene/Alkanethiols: Highly Regioselective Synthesis of β‐Amino Sulfides](../sources/L010.md). The reaction was demonstrated on gram scale and furnished the corresponding product in 75% yield under the standard catalytic conditions. The thiol scope includes substituted benzenethiols, naphthalene-1-thiol, and an alkanethiol; the alkanethiol example required PhI(OAc)2 instead of Cu(OAc)2 and gave 68% yield. The authors propose a Heck-type pathway in which oxidized benzenethiol forms a palladium–sulfur complex, followed by enamine coordination, deprotonation, reduction, elimination, and palladium reoxidation; only the disulfide substitution control directly supports part of this proposal.

- [L039 — Sulfonamide Synthesis through Electrochemical Oxidative Coupling of Amines and Thiols](../sources/L039.md). Batch operation was possible but required 24 hours and increased electrolyte loading, while flow was used in most cases for optimal yields; batch processing was useful when longer reaction times or insoluble starting materials were involved. Acid identity and concentration, electrolyte loading, and anode material affect reaction performance: omitting or reducing acid lowered isolated yields, sulfuric acid was slightly inferior to hydrochloric acid, higher electrolyte concentration lowered yields, and other anode materials were less efficient.

- [L147 — Revealing the unusual role of bases in activation/deactivation of catalytic systems: O–NHC coupling in M/NHC catalysis](../sources/L147.md). Base response depended on metal identity: Pt complexes 5a and 5b were almost unreactive toward KOH in dioxane over 20 h but gave azolone yields of 42% and 65% with tert-BuOK under otherwise matching conditions. The product boundary depended on NHC heterocycle stability: benzimidazole-derived complexes gave the highest azolone yields, whereas most imidazole- and 1,2,4-triazole-derived complexes gave only trace azolones because those products underwent instability and solvolysis.

- [L003 — Nucleophilicities of Nitroalkyl Anions](../sources/L003.md). Solvent strongly changed nitronate nucleophilicity: all nitronates were less nucleophilic in water than in DMSO, with aliphatic nitronates decreasing by roughly a factor of one million, while aromatic nitronates showed a smaller decrease. Consequently, aliphatic nitronates were stronger nucleophiles in DMSO but weaker nucleophiles in water. The authors proposed that hydrogen-bonding solvation of the more localized charges of aliphatic nitronates may explain their particularly large loss of nucleophilicity in water.

- [L037 — Applications of the Controlled Oxidation of Pyrroles](../sources/L037.md). Using a larger excess of Deoxofluor converted fluorohydrin 233 into competing elimination and difluorination products; direct treatment of diol 162 under the discussion conditions gave fluoroalkene 242 and difluorinated product 234 in reported yields of 25% and 20%, respectively. X-ray crystallography established that fluorohydrin 233 resulted from deoxyfluorination at the C3 alcohol with inversion of stereochemistry, leaving fluorine trans to the remaining alcohol.

- [L059 — EPR Spectroscopy as a Tool in Homogeneous Catalysis Research](../sources/L059.md). The review identifies a general mechanistic limitation of EPR: detected paramagnetic species may be inactive species arising from side reactions or catalyst deactivation rather than the catalytically active intermediates. For the chromium trimerization systems discussed, evidence favored participation of Cr(I) species, but a more active EPR-silent species and the competing Cr(II)-Cr(IV) cycle could not be excluded. The authors infer that toluene is not a suitable solvent for the chromium oligomerization systems under discussion.

- [L062 — The poisoning of palladium catalyst for the reaction between hydrogen and oxygen](../sources/L062.md). Stibine gradually poisoned the palladium catalyst, but a stibine-poisoned catalyst regenerated over 120-200 degrees even while stibine remained present. Arsine-poisoned catalyst regenerated completely within 24 hours at 250 degrees after arsine removal, whereas regeneration remained slight after prolonged heating at 280-290 degrees while arsine remained present. The authors attribute recovery from carbon monoxide poisoning under the tested conditions to oxidation of carbon monoxide and note that higher carbon monoxide concentrations would introduce oxygen depletion.

- [L068 — Triphenylphosphine as Efficient Antidote for the Sulfur‐Poisoning of the Pd/C Hydrogenation Catalyst](../sources/L068.md). In the reported dosage study, 0.002-0.03 g TPP, corresponding to 0.4-6% of substrate mass, gave conversion above 90%, whereas 0.1 g TPP gave 42.7%, below the 52.1% TPP-free result; the authors interpreted this as promotion by an appropriate amount and poisoning by excess TPP. The authors attribute TPPS formation and the enhanced high-temperature activity to reduction of palladium sulfide by TPP; at low temperature, they report that TPP did not react with palladium sulfide.

- [L076 — Persulfate-Based Advanced Oxidation: Critical Assessment of Opportunities and Roadblocks](../sources/L076.md). The review finds that reported rapid degradation and pH trends for several substrates contradict established singlet-oxygen kinetics, weakening assignments of singlet oxygen as the primary oxidant. It also distinguishes peroxymonosulfate from peroxydisulfate: peroxymonosulfate is more susceptible to nucleophilic attack and oxygen-atom transfer to anionic nucleophiles, including halides, cyanide, azide and bicarbonate. These observations constrain interpretation of oxidation-based activation and competing reactions.

- [L084 — Identification and Elimination of an Unexpected Catalyst Poison in Suzuki Coupling](../sources/L084.md). A stalled coupling could be rescued with additional catalyst and ligand, but higher catalyst loading increased residual palladium in product 1 and adversely affected downstream reactions. The failed Suzuki coupling closely resembled a control in which Pd(OAc)2 was omitted, initially implicating palladium catalyst poisoning. The authors propose that elemental sulfur contamination most likely arose during an SOCl2/DMF esterification through chemistry involving decomposition of SOCl2 and reaction of SCl2 with DMF.

- [L090 — Revealing the Hidden Complexity and Reactivity of Palladacyclic Precatalysts: The P( o -tolyl) 3 Ligand Enables a Cocktail of Active Species Utilizing the Pd(II)/Pd(IV) and Pd(0)/Pd(II) Pathways for Efficient Catalysis](../sources/L090.md). At fixed reaction time, conversion decreased as the hydroxo-palladacycle loading increased up to 10 mol%, establishing an inverse relationship between observed reaction rate and catalyst concentration under the studied conditions. The authors propose different activation pathways under water-limited and excess-water conditions: limited water favors biaryl formation through a possible bridging aryl or Pd(I) route, whereas excess water is proposed to protonate the transmetalated aryl group before formation of Pd(0).

- [L099 — Inhibition of (dppf)nickel-catalysed Suzuki–Miyaura cross-coupling reactions by α-halo-N-heterocycles](../sources/L099.md). Complex 3-Cl did not react with excess phenylboronic acid and potassium phosphate and was recovered unchanged after treatment with PhSn(n-Bu)3. Alternative nickel precatalysts bearing PPh3, PCy3, dppe, or XantPhos produced less than 2% yield of 2-phenylpyridine in attempted Suzuki–Miyaura coupling of 2-chloropyridine. The authors propose that the butterfly complex can re-enter catalysis only if it transmetalates with a sufficiently nucleophilic organometallic reagent.

- [L114 — Sulfonyl Fluoride Synthesis through Electrochemical Oxidative Coupling of Thiols and Potassium Fluoride](../sources/L114.md). The optimized representative reaction gave the targeted sulfonyl fluoride in 74% isolated yield using KF, pyridine, a biphasic acetonitrile/1 M HCl medium, and graphite/stainless-steel electrodes. A 1-fluoro-pyridinium reagent produced only traces of product, whereas KF combined with either HCl-pyridine or HCl-Et3N gave sulfonyl fluoride in good yields; the authors interpreted this as evidence for nucleophilic fluorination. The authors tentatively assigned observed fluorinated species as sulfenyl fluoride and sulfinyl fluoride intermediates; these species were not isolated.

- [L122 — Effect of 6,6′-Substituents on Bipyridine-Ligated Ni Catalysts for Cross-Electrophile Coupling](../sources/L122.md). The measured precatalyst-activation trends did not follow the electrochemical reduction potentials: although cyclic voltammetry indicated that Me2- and Mes2-substituted complexes were easier to reduce than the unsubstituted complex, only the unsubstituted complex was activated by TME under the catalytic test conditions. The authors infer that, after activation by TDAE, productive coupling fails because formation of or radical trapping by LnNi(Ar)X is too slow relative to alkyl-radical formation and decomposition; proposed radical side reactions include solvent hydrogen-atom abstraction and alkyl–alkyl coupling.

- [L145 — Synthesis of Nickel(I)–Bromide Complexes via Oxidation and Ligand Displacement: Evaluation of Ligand Effects on Speciation and Reactivity](../sources/L145.md). In the studied Suzuki–Miyaura reactions, three tested monophosphines with %Vbur(min) below 32% gave moderate product formation from the NiI precursor, whereas ligands above 32% showed a marked yield decline and the isolated P(t-Bu)3 NiI dimer gave only trace yields. Direct oxidation in the presence of P(t-Bu)3 did not produce the desired NiI dimer; instead, over-oxidized and nickelate products were obtained. The authors propose that the over-oxidized and nickelate products arise through redox equilibrium and ammonium coordination, respectively.

- [L177 — Effects of Reduced Sulfur Compounds on Pd-Catalytic Hydrodechlorination of Trichloroethylene in Groundwater by Cathodic H2under Electrochemically Induced Oxidizing Conditions](../sources/L177.md). Radical-scavenging experiments supported an oxidation pathway parallel to hydrodechlorination: ascorbic acid reduced TCE decay toward the no-sulfite result, while methanol caused only slight inhibition, suggesting little contribution from hydroxyl and sulfate radicals. The proposed mechanism assigns low-sulfite enhancement to SO3 radical anion formation followed by rapid conversion to SO5 radical anion, with both radicals, especially SO5 radical anion, proposed to oxidize TCE intermediates that are subsequently hydrodechlorinated to ethane.

- [L178 — Nucleophilicity Prediction via Multivariate Linear Regression Analysis](../sources/L178.md). Steric descriptors affected modeled nucleophilicity less than electronic descriptors, and the reported steric relationship is specific to reactions with benzhydrylium cations or related quinone methides rather than a general electrophile-independent rule. The authors propose that proton affinity and solvation energies are the principal nucleophilicity descriptors and that thermodynamics is a key contributor to reaction rates, while smaller solvation, steric, and electrostatic terms may affect intrinsic activation energies.

- [L181 — Development of Halogenase Enzymes for Use in Synthesis](../sources/L181.md). A sequential chemoenzymatic example used tryptophan-7-halogenase-generated chloropacidamycin and Suzuki-Miyaura chemistry to prepare 7-aryl pacidamycin derivatives. PrnA and RebH did not accept the tested N-methylated or other N-functionalized tryptophans, whereas SttH accepted N-methylated tryptophan. Reported one-pot systems separated charged biocatalysts and chemocatalysts with hydrophobic PDMS membranes while allowing a nonpolar aryl halide intermediate to diffuse between compartments.

- [L186 — Heterogeneous Gold and Palladium Based Catalysts for Solvent-Free Oxidation of Toluene](../sources/L186.md). Supported Au-Pd/TiO2 catalyzed solvent-free toluene oxidation with TBHP at 80 °C; after 4 hours, the impregnated 5 wt% Au-Pd/TiO2 catalyst gave 3.0% conversion with 27.6% benzyl alcohol, 70.4% benzaldehyde, and 2.1% benzoic acid selectivity, compared with 0.3% conversion in the catalyst-free TBHP reaction. Longer reaction times increased toluene conversion but decreased benzyl alcohol and benzaldehyde selectivity because of consecutive oxidation to benzoic acid.

- [L190 — Branched-Selective Cross-Electrophile Coupling of 2-Alkyl Aziridines and (Hetero)aryl Iodides Using Ti/Ni Catalysis](../sources/L190.md). Control experiments established that omission of Ni and ligand prevented C-C coupling and produced reductive ring-opened products, while omission of Ti gave only 32% aziridine conversion and trace cross-coupled product. The authors propose coupled Ti and Ni cycles in which TiIII coordinates the aziridine and enables reversible radical ring-opening, the resulting radical adds to an arylnickel(II) intermediate, and radical addition to Ni or reductive elimination is likely the regioselectivity-determining step.

- [L198 — Aldehydes and ketones influence reactivity and selectivity in nickel-catalysed Suzuki–Miyaura reactions](../sources/L198.md). In the reported experiments, the nonconjugated ketone-containing substrate 22-Br was not selectively coupled, while intramolecular competition with 23-Cl favored cross-coupling at the aryl chloride site conjugated with the ketone. The directing and inhibitory carbonyl effects were observed with dppe- and Xantphos-supported nickel phosphine complexes as well as dppf, although the alternative complexes performed significantly less well under the tested conditions.

- [L216 — Decomposition of Ruthenium Olefin Metathesis Catalysts](../sources/L216.md). All tested phosphine-based ruthenium methylidene complexes generated methyltricyclohexylphosphonium salts as the major phosphine species upon decomposition. Attempts to prepare the bis(pyridine)-coordinated methylidene complex 24 failed because of instability; its methylidene protons were not observed even in situ. The authors favor decomposition by attack of dissociated free phosphine on the methylidene carbon, but state that the observed kinetics cannot experimentally distinguish this pathway from internal phosphine attack.

- [L217 — Identification of Catalytic Active Sites for Durable Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell: Catalytic Degradation and Poisoning Perspectives](../sources/L217.md). Operando and end-of-test spectroscopy found that two FeNx site populations degraded differently: S1 transformed into iron oxides, whereas S2 remained structurally and numerically unchanged and dominated ORR activity after 50 hours. The active-site configuration of pyrolyzed M-N-C ORR catalysts remains unresolved; metal-coordinated pyridinic nitrogen is favored by most results, but several alternative structures have been suggested and the available data are insufficient for a complete assignment.

- [L220 — Evidence That Protons Can Be the Active Catalysts in Lewis Acid Mediated Hetero‐Michael Addition Reactions](../sources/L220.md). Catalytic activity generally correlated with cation hydrolysis constants: readily hydrolyzing cations gave good yields, borderline cations gave poor conversion, and weakly hydrolyzing cations gave no reaction, with stated rare-earth exceptions. Water loading had a nonmonotonic effect: adding up to two equivalents relative to Pd catalyst accelerated the aza-Michael reaction, whereas larger amounts slowed it because water acted as a Brønsted base.

- [L256 — A review of Ni based powder catalyst for urea oxidation in assisting water splitting reaction](../sources/L256.md). The review reports that urea electrooxidation occurs after formation of Ni(III) species on the electrode surface and that active sites are regenerated during continued adsorption and oxidation. The review identifies Ni-based catalysts as promising for urea oxidation because of cost, structural tunability, compatibility, and facile active-phase formation. A catalyst-regeneration mechanism was proposed in which Ni(OH)2 is electrochemically oxidized to NiOOH, urea is chemically oxidized, and NiOOH is reduced back to Ni(OH)2.

- [L257 — Deactivation Mechanism and Anti-Deactivation Measures of Metal Catalyst in the Dry Reforming of Methane: A Review](../sources/L257.md). In DRM, carbon deposition can deactivate metal catalysts by covering active sites, blocking reactant access, obstructing pores, impairing diffusion, and eventually causing mechanical damage or bed blockage. The paper gives conflicting generalizations about whether noble metals are less or more susceptible to sulfur poisoning than non-noble or transition-metal catalysts. This is a 2023 review article in Atmosphere titled "Deactivation Mechanism and Anti-Deactivation Measures of Metal Catalyst in the Dry Reforming of Methane: A Review," with DOI 10.3390/atmos14050770.

- [L260 — Thiol Ester−Boronic Acid Cross-Coupling. Catalysis Using Alkylative Activation of the Palladium Thiolate Intermediate](../sources/L260.md). The corresponding iodo substrate coupled with phenylboronic acid without NaI to give benzophenone in 56% yield. Without catalytic NaI, the bromobutyl-derived thiol ester produced only traces of benzophenone under otherwise specified palladium-catalyzed conditions. The authors propose that NaI generates the alkyl iodide from the alkyl bromide and suggest that iodide might also form an anionic acylpalladate with increased electron density for alkylative activation.

- [L272 — Resting State and Elementary Steps of the Coupling of Aryl Halides with Thiols Catalyzed by Alkylbisphosphine Complexes of Palladium](../sources/L272.md). Base strongly accelerated reaction of the palladium bis-thiolate complex with an aryl chloride: 99% thioether formed after 40 minutes with NaOtPent, compared with 32% after 25 hours without base. The authors propose that the palladium bis-thiolate reacts through reversible reductive elimination of disulfide followed by irreversible oxidative addition of aryl bromide; the kinetic data are consistent with this pathway.

- [L273 — Mild metal-catalyzed C–H activation: examples and concepts](../sources/L273.md). The review reports mild C–N bond formation with electrophilic amine reagents under Rh, Ru, or Ir catalysis at room temperature to 40 °C without an external oxidant, while describing nucleophilic substitution at nitrogen only as a likely mechanism. The paper is a review of metal-catalyzed C–H activation transformations conducted under comparatively mild conditions, specifically emphasizing reactions at or below ambient temperature, without strongly acidic or basic additives, or without strong oxidants.

- [L278 — Unexpected Nickel Complex Speciation Unlocks Alternative Pathways for the Reactions of Alkyl Halides with dppf-Nickel(0)](../sources/L278.md). Added free dppf significantly accelerated reactions of [Ni(COD)(dppf)] with alkyl halides. With added dppf, the alkyl-halide reactions were pseudo-first-order in the nickel precursor, first-order in alkyl halide and dppf, and inhibited by COD. The authors use the contrasting reactivity of [Ni(dppf)2] and [Ni(dppe)2] to favor a mechanism requiring ligand opening and a vacant coordination site over outer-sphere electron transfer.

- [L283 — A Dichotomy in Cross-Coupling Site Selectivity in a Dihalogenated Heteroarene: Influence of Mononuclear Pd, Pd Clusters, and Pd Nanoparticles—the Case for Exploiting Pd Catalyst Speciation](../sources/L283.md). With Pd(PPh3)4, direct reaction with 2,4-dibromopyridine predominantly formed the C2 oxidative-addition regioisomer at 23 °C, and the major regioisomer was characterized by X-ray diffraction and NMR spectroscopy. For Pd3-cluster or nanoparticle catalysis, the authors propose organoboron activation before aryl-halide activation, potentially through a Pd3-hydroxo species or a boronate complex activated at a nanoparticle surface; they explicitly state that the mechanistic model requires independent investigation.

- [L289 — Quantitative Differences in Sulfur Poisoning Phenomena over Ruthenium and Palladium: An Attempt To Deconvolute Geometric and Electronic Poisoning Effects Using Model Catalysts](../sources/L289.md). For Pd(PVP), first-stage alkyne hydrogenation showed no significant coverage-dependent rate change, including at theoretical MEA coverages above one monolayer, and cis-alkene selectivity remained above 94% across the reported coverage range. Sulfur modification progressively inhibited the examined second-stage Pd-catalyzed conversion of cis-3-hexen-1-ol; 30% sulfur coverage caused more than a 70% reduction in initial activity, and the rate of further deactivation slowed beyond approximately 55%–60% coverage.

- [L303 — Mechanistic Studies of Palladium-Catalyzed Aminocarbonylation of Aryl Chlorides with Carbon Monoxide and Ammonia](../sources/L303.md). Oxidative addition of aryl chlorides and bromides was determined to proceed through rate-limiting reaction of the haloarene with a three-coordinate Pd(0) species bearing a bidentate phosphine and one CO ligand. Arylpalladium and phenacylpalladium halide intermediates were synthesized, and their formation and reactions were studied kinetically. A primary carbon-13 kinetic isotope effect was interpreted as suggesting carbon-halogen bond cleavage during the rate-limiting oxidative-addition step.

- [L304 — In Situ Studies of Arylboronic Acids/Esters and R3SiCF3 Reagents: Kinetics, Speciation, and Dysfunction at the Carbanion–Ate Interface](../sources/L304.md). For CF3 addition to the ketone, excess ketone produced autoacceleration, whereas excess TMSCF3 progressively slowed the reaction; O-silylation of enolizable ketones was the only major side reaction reported. Kinetic analysis supports a mechanism in which the siliconate serves as a reservoir and TMSCF3 inhibits CF3 transfer by sequestering free trifluoromethanide; calculations disfavor direct CF3 transfer from the siliconate and support predissociation of CF3 anion.

- [L308 — Inhibition of a Gold-Based Catalyst in Benzyl Alcohol Oxidation: Understanding and Remediation](../sources/L308.md). A small amount of water used with K2CO3 prevented observable catalyst deactivation and gave conversion close to 100%, but water alone did not restore the deactivated catalyst; excessive water caused phase separation, catalyst agglomeration, and mass-transport limitation. Pre-addition of benzoic acid caused severe inhibition: despite introducing about 40 times less acid than aldehyde in the comparison experiment, hardly any benzyl alcohol conversion occurred, and only about 25% of the added acid was detected in solution.

- [L331 — Elucidating the reaction mechanism of a palladium-palladium dual catalytic process through kinetic studies of proposed elementary steps](../sources/L331.md). The paper experimentally studies a palladium-catalysed C-C cross-coupling of aryl halides with terminal alkynes by separating a proposed dual-palladium mechanism into independently investigated elementary reactions. The authors propose a dissociative Pd-Pd transmetallation pathway initiated by PPh3 loss, followed by association through a cyclic intermediate containing bridging alkyne and iodide ligands; dissociation of a later Pd-Pd complex is proposed as the most likely rate-determining step of that stoichiometric process.

- [L332 — Comparison of Monophosphine and Bisphosphine Precatalysts for Ni-Catalyzed Suzuki–Miyaura Cross-Coupling: Understanding the Role of the Ligation State in Catalysis](../sources/L332.md). With 2-thiophenylboronic acid, added thiophene or increased boronic-acid loading decreased the yield of the monophosphine system, whereas added 2-phenylthiophene product had no effect and the bisphosphine systems were unaffected by these additives. The authors propose that thiophene formed by protodeboronation deactivates the more labile monophosphine system, while P2Ni-favoring bisphosphines protect the catalyst; direct quantification of thiophene and discrimination between catalyst-mediated and base-catalyzed formation were unsuccessful.

- [L341 — Palladium-Catalyzed Carbothiolation of Alkenes and Alkynes for the Synthesis of Heterocycles](../sources/L341.md). The reaction scope was examined using 25 mol% IPr because lower ligand loading caused decreased yield under several tested conditions. Alkyne products showed isomerization behavior: N-acetyl derivative 46 was observed only as the isomerized indole derivative in the crude mixture, while product 47 partially isomerized during silica-gel chromatography. The authors propose that reversible C-S oxidative addition produces self-correcting selectivity, and they regard formation of cyclized amide 41 in 87% yield as support for that proposal.
