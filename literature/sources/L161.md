# L161 — Kinetics of electrophile-nucleophile combinations: A general approach to polar organic reactivity

**Online reference 253; Tier 3.**

Herbert Mayr; Armin R. Ofial. Kinetics of electrophile-nucleophile combinations: A general approach to polar organic reactivity. Pure and Applied Chemistry 77(11), 1807-1821 (2005). https://doi.org/10.1351/pac200577111807

[doi:10.1351/pac200577111807](https://doi.org/10.1351/pac200577111807)

## Chemical contribution

For trivalent phosphorus nucleophiles, a predicted fast initial electrophile–nucleophile combination does not necessarily yield a macroscopic phosphonium-product-forming reaction because initially formed phosphonium ions may redissociate when no irreversible follow-up reaction is available. The authors propose that E + N > -5 predicts whether an electrophile–nucleophile combination can occur at 20 °C and that s(N + E) > 9 predicts diffusion control, which they associate with loss of selectivity.

### L161-S001

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

A single reference electrophile cannot rank the full nucleophile range because highly reactive nucleophiles can converge on diffusion-controlled rates while weak, nonactivated arenes and alkenes may not react at measurable rates at 20 °C.

### L161-S003

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Carbanion nucleophilicity can change substantially and nonuniformly with solvent; ester- and keto-stabilized carbanions were about three N units more nucleophilic in DMSO than water, whereas the malonodinitrile anion was nearly unaffected.

Location: 8; Solvent effects on carbanion nucleophilicity

### L161-S004

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The reference-scale kinetics were measured with 29 benzhydrylium ions or related quinone methides and varied carbon nucleophiles, generally in dichloromethane for carbocations plus neutral nucleophiles or DMSO for anions plus neutral or positively charged electrophiles, by monitoring electrophile consumption photometrically.

Location: 2; Construction of reactivity scales for electrophiles and nucleophiles

### L161-S005

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors use the empirical correlation log k (20 °C) = s(N + E), where N and s are nucleophile-dependent parameters and E is an electrophilicity parameter, to compare polar electrophile–nucleophile reactivity.

Location: 1; Abstract; definition and intended scope of the reactivity correlation

### L161-S006

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Pairing stronger electrophiles with weaker nucleophiles and weaker electrophiles with stronger nucleophiles produced measured second-order rate constants from 10^-5 to 5 × 10^7 M^-1 s^-1 at 20 °C.

Location: 2; Construction of reactivity scales; experimentally covered rate range

### L161-S007

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Across the broad reactivity scales, calculated and experimental rate constants generally agreed within a factor of 100; larger deviations were observed for very bulky reagents or when the mechanism changed.

Location: 11; Accuracy and limitations of the empirical reactivity correlation
