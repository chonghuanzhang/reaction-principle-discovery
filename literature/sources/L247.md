# L247 — Photocatalytic defluoroalkylation and hydrodefluorination of trifluoromethyls using o-phosphinophenolate

**Online reference 323; Tier 2.**

Can Liu; Ni Shen; Rui Shang. Photocatalytic defluoroalkylation and hydrodefluorination of trifluoromethyls using o-phosphinophenolate. Nature Communications 13(1), 354 (2022). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28007-2

[doi:10.1038/s41467-022-28007-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-28007-2)

## Chemical contribution

The electrophile scope extended beyond trifluoroacetamides to trifluoroacetates and trifluoromethyl (hetero)arenes, although trifluoromethylbenzene itself showed less than 15% conversion. The authors propose that photoexcited PO− reduces the trifluoromethyl substrate to a difluoromethyl radical, which either undergoes hydrogen-donor reduction or alkene capture; the resulting alkyl radical is then proposed to be reduced through thiol HAT catalysis, with formate-derived CO2 radical anion participating in catalyst turnover.

### L247-S001

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Trifluoroacetamide substitution strongly affected feasibility and selectivity: electron-neutral or electron-rich N-aryl substrates mainly gave monodefluoroalkylation products with 5–10% hydrodefluorination, strongly electron-deficient N-aryl substrates mainly gave didefluoroalkylation, an N-aryl substituent was essential, and tertiary amides were not applicable.

### L247-S005

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The reported spectra identify PO1− as the visible-light-absorbing species in the reaction mixture; its excited-state reduction potential was estimated as −2.89 V versus SCE, and no bathochromic shift consistent with formation of an electron-donor-acceptor complex with amide 1 was observed.

Location: 2; Results and discussion: photophysical properties of PO1−

### L247-S006

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Control experiments showed that PO1, 1-adamantanethiol, and light were essential for the reported reactivity; omission of formate still gave product 3 in 18% yield, whereas exposure to air completely suppressed catalytic reactivity and resulted in recovery of starting materials.

Location: 3; Results and discussion: reaction controls and oxygen sensitivity

<a id="statement-l247-s007"></a>

### L247-S007

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The general trifluoroacetamide defluoroalkylation procedure used trifluoroacetamide, 1.5 equivalents of alkene, 10 mol% PO1, 30 mol% Cs2CO3, 20 mol% 1-adamantanethiol, and 2 equivalents of potassium formate in anhydrous DMA under argon, with 427 nm irradiation at room temperature for 24 hours.

Location: 6; Methods: general procedure for defluoroalkylation of trifluoroacetamides

<a id="statement-l247-s008"></a>

### L247-S008

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

A catalytic o-phosphinophenolate/thiol HAT system effected monoselective defluoroalkylation of N-phenyltrifluoroacetamide with alkenes under 427 nm irradiation; under the reported optimal conditions, the desired product was obtained in 90% NMR yield with 5% of byproduct 4.

Location: 2; Results and discussion: discovery and optimization of trifluoroacetamide defluoroalkylation

<a id="statement-l247-s009"></a>

### L247-S009

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Monoselectivity was condition- and substrate-dependent: increasing alkene loading suppressed hydrodefluorination, cesium formate loading was critical for hydrodefluorination monoselectivity, and a para-ester-substituted N-phenyltrifluoroacetamide underwent complete hydrodefluorination.

Location: 3; Condition and substrate boundaries for mono- versus further defluorination
