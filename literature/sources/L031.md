# L031 — Defluorination of HFCs by a magnesium reagent

**SI reference 147; Tier 2.**

Daniel J. Sheldon; Joseph M. Parr; Mark R. Crimmin. Defluorination of HFCs by a magnesium reagent. Dalton Transactions 53(15), 6524-6528 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1039/d4dt00636d

[doi:10.1039/d4dt00636d](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4dt00636d)

## Chemical contribution

For 1,1-difluoroethane and 1,1,1-trifluoroethane, the organic products and the fate of the organic fragments were not established; no obvious fluorine-containing volatile species or isolable residue products beyond complex 2 were found. Based on the absence of identified alkene products, the authors propose that fluorine substitution on both ethane carbon atoms may be required for the 1,2-elimination pathway; otherwise, organomagnesium intermediates may decompose by α-elimination to unstable carbene fragments.

### L031-S001

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors suggest that the minor α,β,β-trifluorostyrene-d5 may arise by defluorinative coupling with benzene-d6 solvent, but state that its pathway is unclear and that a fluoroethene intermediate cannot be ruled out.

Location: 2; Hypothesized origin of the HFC-125 minor product

### L031-S002

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Additional HFCs underwent reaction with 1 and DMAP, but condition and yield requirements varied: HFC-125 gave complex 2 in 59% yield after five days at 25 °C; 1,1-difluoroethane required 60 °C for completion and gave 2 in 41% yield; and 1,1,1-trifluoroethane formed 2 after 16 hours at 60 °C.

Location: 2; Experimental HFC substrate comparison

### L031-S005

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

No magnesium hydride side-products attributed to sp3 C-H activation were observed for the additional HFC substrates, supporting chemoselective sp3 C-F reaction under the reported conditions.

Location: 2; Observed chemoselectivity boundary

<a id="statement-l031-s006"></a>

### L031-S006

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors calculated barriers of 27.1 kcal mol−1 for sp3 C-H deprotonation and 26.5 kcal mol−1 for attack at a fluorine atom of the CF3 group; they also reported that they could not locate a suitable transition state for concerted cleavage of two sp3 C-F bonds.

Location: 1; Computational chemoselectivity and alternative pathways

<a id="statement-l031-s007"></a>

### L031-S007

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

At 25 °C in C6D6 with approximately seven equivalents of HFC-134a, magnesium reagent 1, and two equivalents of DMAP for 16 hours, the authors observed magnesium fluoride complex 2 in 86% yield and 1,1-difluoroethene; the volatile alkene was not quantified.

Location: 1; Experimental defluorination of HFC-134a

<a id="statement-l031-s008"></a>

### L031-S008

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The 1,1-difluoroethene produced from HFC-134a was separated from complex 2 by vacuum transfer and underwent downstream defluorosilylation with lithium silanide 4 to give organosilicon compound 5 in 40% NMR yield over two steps, based on reagent 1 as the limiting reagent.

Location: 2; Downstream nucleophilic substitution of the fluoroalkene product

<a id="statement-l031-s009"></a>

### L031-S009

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Reaction of HFC-125 with 1 and DMAP produced a minor fluorinated alkene product identified as α,β,β-trifluorostyrene-d5 in 5–10% yield based on one equivalent of reagent 1.

Location: 2; Minor product from HFC-125

<a id="statement-l031-s010"></a>

### L031-S010

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors' DFT-based mechanism proposes initial attack of 1·DMAP at the CH2F-site sp3 C-F bond through a 19.5 kcal mol−1 barrier to form Int-1, followed by fluoride elimination through a 6.6 kcal mol−1 barrier to form 1,1-difluoroethene.

Location: 1; DFT-proposed pathway for HFC-134a defluorination

<a id="statement-l031-s011"></a>

### L031-S011

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

DMAP materially changes the required conditions: defluorination without DMAP required heating to 80 °C and gave 30% yield of a DMAP-free analogue of complex 2.

Location: 1; Effect of the Lewis base additive DMAP
