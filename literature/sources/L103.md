# L103 — Radicals as Exceptional Electron-Withdrawing Groups: Nucleophilic Aromatic Substitution of Halophenols Via Homolysis-Enabled Electronic Activation

**Online reference 207; Tier 3.**

Nick Y. Shin; Elaine Tsui; Adam Reinhold; Gregory D. Scholes; Matthew J. Bird; Robert R. Knowles. Radicals as Exceptional Electron-Withdrawing Groups: Nucleophilic Aromatic Substitution of Halophenols Via Homolysis-Enabled Electronic Activation. Journal of the American Chemical Society 144(47), 21783-21790 (2022). https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c10296

[doi:10.1021/jacs.2c10296](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.2c10296)

## Chemical contribution

The reaction was also demonstrated with 4-chloro-2,6-dimethylphenol and multiple aryl and alkyl carboxylates. Fluorophenols lacking ortho substituents did not furnish the desired products; the authors attributed this limitation, tentatively, to competitive phenoxyl-radical dimerization. The authors propose a radical-chain mechanism initiated by base/Fe(III) proton-transfer/electron-transfer formation of a phenoxyl radical, followed by carboxylate SNAr and hydrogen-atom exchange that regenerates the chain-carrying phenoxyl radical.
