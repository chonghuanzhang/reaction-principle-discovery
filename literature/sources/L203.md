# L203 — Defluorinative Coupling of (NH)-Heteroarenes and Unactivated Vinyl Fluorides Enabled by Metal–Organic Frameworks

**Online reference 62; Tier 2.**

Kaitlyn T. Keasler; Jaehwan Kim; Julia C. Pitolaj; Phillip J. Milner. Defluorinative Coupling of (NH)-Heteroarenes and Unactivated Vinyl Fluorides Enabled by Metal–Organic Frameworks. Journal of the American Chemical Society 147(36), 32776-32785 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5c08791

[doi:10.1021/jacs.5c08791](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5c08791)

## Chemical contribution

Direct VDF delivery performs substantially worse and less reproducibly than VDF–Mg2(dobdc): balloon delivery gave 47 ± 7% yield and a VDF-saturated DMSO solution gave 61%, whereas the MOF reagent consistently furnished quantitative yield. The authors propose that the high yields with VDF–Mg2(dobdc) arise from a combination of Lewis acidity and reservoir-mediated VDF delivery; the supplied evidence does not independently establish the precise catalytic role of surface versus leached Mg2+ sites.

### L203-S007

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The optimized model reaction uses 1H-pyrazole, 4.1 equivalents of VDF–Mg2(dobdc) (approximately 8 equivalents VDF), 1.2 equivalents K3PO4, DMSO, and 60 °C, affording the N-fluorovinylated product in quantitative yield; Table 1 specifies a 20-hour reaction time.

### L203-S008

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

For heteroarenes with multiple nonequivalent nitrogen atoms, fluorovinylation generally occurs at the least sterically hindered nitrogen. An amino-substituted indazole reacts at the indazole nitrogen while retaining its amino group, whereas hydroxy- and hydroxymethyl-substituted indazoles can undergo both O- and N-fluorovinylation.

Location: 5; Results and Discussion: regioselectivity and competition among N- and O-nucleophilic sites.

<a id="statement-l203-s009"></a>

### L203-S009

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

HFP–Mg2(dobdc) also undergoes defluorinative coupling with (NH)-heteroarenes at room temperature. Addition favors the less hindered alkene carbon and the trans product modestly, while further reaction of HFP with product generates an over-substituted side product that can be suppressed by portion-wise HFP–MOF addition.

Location: 8; Results and Discussion: extension to HFP, selectivity, and over-substitution boundary.

### L203-S010

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Free (NH)-heteroarenes react directly with vinylidene fluoride through a defluorinative pathway to give terminal N-fluorovinylated products, establishing C-N bond formation with retention of one fluorine on the alkene.
