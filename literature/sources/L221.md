# L221 — Selectivity in organocatalysis—From qualitative to quantitative predictive models

**SI reference 301; Tier 3.**

Alistair J. Sterling; Stamatia Zavitsanou; Joseph Ford; Fernanda Duarte. Selectivity in organocatalysis—From qualitative to quantitative predictive models. WIREs Computational Molecular Science 11(5), e1518 (2021). https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.1518

[doi:10.1002/wcms.1518](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcms.1518)

## Chemical contribution

A framework combining calculated free-energy differences with empirical electrophilicity and nucleophilicity parameters can classify reactions as thermodynamically, diffusion, or activation controlled. Classifying selectivity only through steric, charge-controlled, or orbital-controlled processes has limitations because it omits London dispersion and assumes that kinetics of a single elementary step determine selectivity. Selectivity may be controlled by kinetics, thermodynamics, or both, including Curtin-Hammett control with rapidly interconverting intermediates.
