# L148 — Application of HSAB Theory in the Selective Reduction of Quinoline

**SI reference 245; Tier 3.**

Tianwei Liu; Jianghua He; Yuetao Zhang. Application of HSAB Theory in the Selective Reduction of Quinoline. Chinese Journal of Chemistry 41(19), 2446-2452 (2023). https://doi.org/10.1002/cjoc.202300124

[doi:10.1002/cjoc.202300124](https://doi.org/10.1002/cjoc.202300124)

## Chemical contribution

The reaction accommodated quinolines bearing electron-donating methyl substituents and electron-withdrawing halogen substituents, with reported yields ranging from 62% to 99%; the C4-methyl substrate required 24 hours rather than 2 hours. The authors propose that HBcat first forms adduct 2 with quinoline; B(C6F5)3 then mediates hydride abstraction and transfer to C2 or C4 to give N-borylated dihydroquinoline intermediates, after which B(C6F5)3-activated PhSiH3 reacts with their C=C bonds to form the final product.

### L148-S008

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors propose that insufficiently positive silicon centers cannot attack the C3 sites of the N-boryl-dihydroquinoline intermediates, and that the electron-withdrawing chloride in Ph2SiClH might weaken B(C6F5)3-mediated silane-hydride activation.

Location: 3; Results and Discussion: proposed explanation for unsuccessful hydrosilanes

### L148-S009

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

In situ 1H NMR experiments identified formation of a Lewis acid-base adduct from HBcat and quinoline, complete recovery of PhSiH3 in the corresponding three-component mixture, and rapid conversion of the adduct to an N-borylated dihydroquinoline after addition of catalytic B(C6F5)3.

Location: 3; Mechanism insight: in situ NMR observations
