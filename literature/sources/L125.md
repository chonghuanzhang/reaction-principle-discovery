# L125 — Metal Enolates – Enamines – Enol Ethers: How Do Enolate Equivalents Differ in Nucleophilic Reactivity?

**SI reference 228; Tier 3.**

Artem Leonov; Daria Timofeeva; Armin Ofial; Herbert Mayr. Metal Enolates – Enamines – Enol Ethers: How Do Enolate Equivalents Differ in Nucleophilic Reactivity? Synthesis 51(05), 1157-1170 (2019). https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0037-1611634

[doi:10.1055/s-0037-1611634](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0037-1611634)

## Chemical contribution

Less reactive electrophiles, including carbonyl compounds, α,β-unsaturated ketones, and alkyl acrylates, require activation for reactions with silyl enol ethers. Substitution at the electrophilic attack site strongly reduced silyl enol ether nucleophilicity: methyl substitution caused a tenfold decrease, while phenyl substitution caused a three-order-of-magnitude decrease. The authors propose that Lewis-base activation can involve an anion coordinating to silicon and forming a hypervalent silicon species that enhances silyl enol ether nucleophilicity.
