# L145 — Synthesis of Nickel(I)–Bromide Complexes via Oxidation and Ligand Displacement: Evaluation of Ligand Effects on Speciation and Reactivity

**SI reference 243; Tier 4.**

Samuel H. Newman-Stonebraker; T. Judah Raab; Hootan Roshandel; Abigail G. Doyle. Synthesis of Nickel(I)–Bromide Complexes via Oxidation and Ligand Displacement: Evaluation of Ligand Effects on Speciation and Reactivity. Journal of the American Chemical Society 145(35), 19368-19377 (2023). https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c06233

[doi:10.1021/jacs.3c06233](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.3c06233)

## Chemical contribution

In the studied Suzuki–Miyaura reactions, three tested monophosphines with %Vbur(min) below 32% gave moderate product formation from the NiI precursor, whereas ligands above 32% showed a marked yield decline and the isolated P(t-Bu)3 NiI dimer gave only trace yields. Direct oxidation in the presence of P(t-Bu)3 did not produce the desired NiI dimer; instead, over-oxidized and nickelate products were obtained. The authors propose that the over-oxidized and nickelate products arise through redox equilibrium and ammonium coordination, respectively.

### L145-S005

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors suggest that bulky monophosphine-bound NiI complexes above the 32% buried-volume threshold are less able to re-enter the Ni0/NiII Suzuki–Miyaura cycle and that binding at least two phosphines is important for stabilizing the even-oxidation-state species.

Location: 3; Authors' mechanistic interpretation of the Suzuki–Miyaura ligand threshold.
