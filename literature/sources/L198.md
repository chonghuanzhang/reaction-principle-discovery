# L198 — Aldehydes and ketones influence reactivity and selectivity in nickel-catalysed Suzuki–Miyaura reactions

**SI reference 282; Tier 4.**

Alasdair K. Cooper; David K. Leonard; Sonia Bajo; Paul M. Burton; David J. Nelson. Aldehydes and ketones influence reactivity and selectivity in nickel-catalysed Suzuki–Miyaura reactions. Chemical Science 11(7), 1905-1911 (2020). https://doi.org/10.1039/c9sc05444h

[doi:10.1039/c9sc05444h](https://doi.org/10.1039/c9sc05444h)

## Chemical contribution

In the reported experiments, the nonconjugated ketone-containing substrate 22-Br was not selectively coupled, while intramolecular competition with 23-Cl favored cross-coupling at the aryl chloride site conjugated with the ketone. The directing and inhibitory carbonyl effects were observed with dppe- and Xantphos-supported nickel phosphine complexes as well as dppf, although the alternative complexes performed significantly less well under the tested conditions.

### L198-S010

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Exogenous aldehyde and ketone additives generally inhibited the model nickel-catalyzed cross-coupling; acetophenone was an exception, while one equivalent of 2,2,2-trifluoroacetophenone almost completely stopped the reaction.

Location: 2; Catalyst inhibition robustness screen

### L198-S011

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Measured COD-displacement equilibria varied substantially among benzaldehyde, benzophenone, and acetophenone, supporting strong substrate-dependent differences in carbonyl coordination to Ni(0).

### L198-S012

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors interpret the robustness-screen results as consistent with inhibition caused by exogenous carbonyl compounds coordinating to and sequestering the Ni(0) catalyst, rather than additive consumption tracking the inhibition.
