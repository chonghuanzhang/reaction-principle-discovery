# L061 — Oxidation of phenolic acid surrogates and target analytes during acid extraction of natural water samples for analysis by GC/MS using EPA method 625

**SI reference 172; Tier 1.**

Paul H. Chen; William A. VanAusdale; Dwight F. Roberts. Oxidation of phenolic acid surrogates and target analytes during acid extraction of natural water samples for analysis by GC/MS using EPA method 625. Environmental Science & Technology 25(3), 540-546 (1991). https://doi.org/10.1021/es00015a025

[doi:10.1021/es00015a025](https://doi.org/10.1021/es00015a025)

## Chemical contribution

Changing the order of extraction so that acidic extraction preceded alkaline extraction gave good recoveries for all tested phenols. For 2,4-dimethylphenol, recovery depended strongly on extraction pH: only 2.6% was recovered at pH 7, whereas initial extraction at pH 10 gave high recovery in the presence of oxidizing agents. The identified products accounted for only a small fraction of the lost surrogates; the authors proposed formation of acid-soluble, nonextractable, nonvolatile, or polymeric oxidation products as possible explanations.

### L061-S007

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

The reported oxidation problem could be eliminated by adding sodium thiosulfate before extraction, or by extraction at pH <2 alone or initial extraction at pH 2 followed by pH >11, provided manganese(III,IV) oxide particulates were not present before acid extraction; sodium thiosulfate occasionally produced large amounts of S8 under strongly acidic conditions.

Location: 7; Summary and Conclusions: mitigation strategies and limitations
