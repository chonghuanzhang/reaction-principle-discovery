# L059 — EPR Spectroscopy as a Tool in Homogeneous Catalysis Research

**SI reference 170; Tier 4.**

Monalisa Goswami; Andrei Chirila; Christophe Rebreyend; Bas de Bruin. EPR Spectroscopy as a Tool in Homogeneous Catalysis Research. Topics in Catalysis 58(12-13), 719-750 (2015). https://doi.org/10.1007/s11244-015-0414-9

[doi:10.1007/s11244-015-0414-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11244-015-0414-9)

## Chemical contribution

The review identifies a general mechanistic limitation of EPR: detected paramagnetic species may be inactive species arising from side reactions or catalyst deactivation rather than the catalytically active intermediates. For the chromium trimerization systems discussed, evidence favored participation of Cr(I) species, but a more active EPR-silent species and the competing Cr(II)-Cr(IV) cycle could not be excluded. The authors infer that toluene is not a suitable solvent for the chromium oligomerization systems under discussion.
