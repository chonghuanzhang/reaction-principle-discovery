# L065 — The Key Role of GSH in Keeping the Redox Balance in Mammalian Cells: Mechanisms and Significance of GSH in Detoxification via Formation of Conjugates

**SI reference 176; Tier 3.**

Sofia K. Georgiou-Siafis; Asterios S. Tsiftsoglou. The Key Role of GSH in Keeping the Redox Balance in Mammalian Cells: Mechanisms and Significance of GSH in Detoxification via Formation of Conjugates. Antioxidants 12(11), 1953 (2023). https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox12111953

[doi:10.3390/antiox12111953](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox12111953)

## Chemical contribution

GSH directly reacts as a nucleophile with electrophilic compounds to form thioether GSH S-conjugates. GSH conjugation is not uniformly detoxifying: some conjugates are unstable or participate in subsequent reactions that produce toxic products. Reported electrophile classes for GSH conjugation include Michael acceptors, epoxides, electron-withdrawing structures, and carbonyls; reaction modes include nucleophilic addition, substitution, and displacement.
