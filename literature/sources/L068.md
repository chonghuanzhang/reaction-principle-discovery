# L068 — Triphenylphosphine as Efficient Antidote for the Sulfur‐Poisoning of the Pd/C Hydrogenation Catalyst

**SI reference 178; Tier 4.**

Renjie Xiong; Wenqiang Ren; Zhiqiang Wang; Minghui Zhang. Triphenylphosphine as Efficient Antidote for the Sulfur‐Poisoning of the Pd/C Hydrogenation Catalyst. ChemCatChem 13(2), 548-552 (2021). https://doi.org/10.1002/cctc.202001537

[doi:10.1002/cctc.202001537](https://doi.org/10.1002/cctc.202001537)

## Chemical contribution

In the reported dosage study, 0.002-0.03 g TPP, corresponding to 0.4-6% of substrate mass, gave conversion above 90%, whereas 0.1 g TPP gave 42.7%, below the 52.1% TPP-free result; the authors interpreted this as promotion by an appropriate amount and poisoning by excess TPP. The authors attribute TPPS formation and the enhanced high-temperature activity to reduction of palladium sulfide by TPP; at low temperature, they report that TPP did not react with palladium sulfide.

### L068-S001

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

TPP broadly improved Pd/C hydrogenation performance in the presence of several organic sulfur poisons; for thioanisole, conversion increased from 8.5% without TPP to 100% with TPP, while the reported selectivities were 98.7% and 99.9%, respectively.

Location: 4; Table 1 scope across different organic sulfur poisons.

### L068-S002

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

XPS peak fitting assigned S1 to sulfur in palladium sulfide; its fraction of total sulfur was lower in the TPP-containing system than in the TPP-free system, at 11% versus 15% at 100 °C and 23% versus 33% at 170 °C.

Location: 3; XPS comparison of sulfur species on used Pd/C with and without TPP.

### L068-S005

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

In the reported recycling study, TPP was added for the next run at the same TPP-to-catalyst ratio as in the first run, and 4-nitrothioanisole conversion was still 98.9% after five runs.

Location: 3; Five-run stability study of Pd/C with replenished TPP.

### L068-S006

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

TPP had a negative effect below 120 °C, whereas above 140 °C it sharply increased Pd/C activity; 4-nitrothioanisole conversion reached 91.7% at 150 °C and almost 100% at 170 °C in the presence of TPP.

Location: 2; Temperature dependence of the TPP effect in sulfur-containing Pd/C hydrogenation.

<a id="statement-l068-s007"></a>

### L068-S007

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Adding a catalytic amount of TPP at a TPP-to-substrate mass ratio of 0.6% in the fifth run restored conversion of 4-nitrothioanisole to approximately 100% over previously deactivated Pd/C.

Location: 2; Catalyst-reactivation experiment in the recycling series.

<a id="statement-l068-s008"></a>

### L068-S008

**AUTHOR HYPOTHESIS**; candidate-generation evidence.

The authors suggest that TPP poisons Pd/C through strong coordination with palladium species and is a stronger poison than thiophene or 4-nitrothioanisole.

Location: 2; Interpretation of the negligible hydrogenation conversion observed after adding TPP.

<a id="statement-l068-s009"></a>

### L068-S009

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Repeated hydrogenation of 4-nitrothioanisole progressively deactivated Pd/C, with the catalyst almost losing activity after the fourth recycle.

Location: 2; Recycling experiment used to establish progressive sulfur poisoning of Pd/C.

### L068-S011

**LITERATURE FACT**; candidate-generation evidence.

Under the tested Pd/C hydrogenation conditions, conversion of 4-nitroanisole decreased to 51.0%, decreased further to 16.5% when 4-nitrothioanisole was used as the poison, and was negligible with TPP; the quotation does not explicitly identify thiophene as the cause of the 51.0% result.

Location: 2; Initial comparison of sulfur compounds and TPP as poisons in Pd/C-catalyzed hydrogenation of 4-nitroanisole.
